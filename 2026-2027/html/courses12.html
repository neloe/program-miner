Mass Media
  

  • MMED 20327 - Advanced Reporting

    Credit Hours: 3
    Theory and practice of reporting for a variety of media. Students will develop important skills in interviewing, computer-assisted reporting and in covering special beats. Students will continue to develop their ability to correctly use Associated Press broadcast and print style. Students will produce content for newspaper, television and online coverage.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20227  and MMED 20315 .
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20329 - Audio Technology and Recording

    Credit Hours: 3
    An examination of audio technology and recording techniques as they apply to studio production. Students will explore the theory of audio technology from the past to the present through studio and live multi-track recording, editing and mixing sessions. Lecture and Laboratory.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20224 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20330 - Advanced Video Production

    Credit Hours: 3
    Advanced study and development of video production skills. Specific areas of study include electronic field production, post production, lighting and set design.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and MMED 20225 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20333 - Social Media Strategies

    Credit Hours: 3
    The study and application of social media tools and theories as they apply to the development of a social media strategy as employed by media and/or creative organizations.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced Standing.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20334 - UX and Analytics

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will examine user experience (UX) best practices and common tools used in early-state user experience planning. Emphasis will be on both user intent and analytics data to make UX improvements to digital properties.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20243  and MMED 20303 .
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20343 - Communication Design

    Credit Hours: 3
    Study of advanced design techniques and principles used for print and multimedia design. Various software packages, design trends and readability issues will be examined.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20243 
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20345 - Sports Broadcasting

    Credit Hours: 3
    Sports Broadcasting will apply the student’s current multimedia journalism skills and experience to the specialized field of sports coverage. The student will explore the history of broadcast sports coverage as well as the direction in which the field is headed. He or she will critically review journalistic ethics as they apply to sports coverage. The student will use interviewing, writing, audio and video as storytelling tools. The student will also develop basic skills associated with sports play-by-play.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20315 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20350 - Advanced Journalism Practicum (Yearbook)

    Credit Hours: 2
    Advanced laboratory practice working on the Tower student yearbook.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and MMED 20150 .
    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for a new, advanced or expanded experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • MMED 20351 - Advanced Journalism Practicum (Newspaper)

    Credit Hours: 2
    Advanced laboratory practice working on the Northwest Missourian student newspaper.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and MMED 20151 .
    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for a new, advanced or expanded experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • MMED 20352 - Copy Editing

    Credit Hours: 3
    Theory and practice of news processing, i.e. copy editing, coaching, grammar and news structure.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20227 
    Typically Offered: SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20355 - Photojournalism

    Credit Hours: 3
    This is an introductory course in visual journalistic communication that will help students develop the photographic skills needed to become successful at producing images and recognizing what makes a good photograph in terms of content, composition and technical quality. Instruction will progress from basic camera operation and the news one shot to more comprehensive visual storytelling that may incorporate video or other multimedia components.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20356 - Sports Photography

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will concentrate heavily on covering sports events here on campus and also in the area. Various sports will first be examined and example photos will be viewed in class. Students will then be expected to photograph and file photos under realistic deadline situations. Students will also be introduced to web-based multimedia production methods for the sports website.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced Standing.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20357 - Opinion Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    Opinion Writing emphasizes writing and critical thinking in the development of institutional and personal editorials and columns for newspapers and other print media. The mission, obligation and history of opinion writing and the editorial page will be covered, along with correct and effective use of the English language to produce persuasive, argumentative, explanatory and informational editorials. Current issues will serve as springboards for comment.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20227 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20358 - Feature Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    Writing and revising articles for magazines, writing queries, personality sketches and educational features.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20359 - Sports Reporting

    Credit Hours: 3
    Comprehensive preparation in identifying, gathering, structuring and styling sports news in a variety of formats.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20227 
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20370 - Live Sound Theory and Practice

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course examines the basic theoretical and practical elements of live sound. Equipment, technology, performance management, production roles, sound theory, and sound measurement and analysis are covered. Lecture and Laboratory.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20224  and MMED 20329 , or consent of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20400 - Special Offering

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Courses which are offered on only one occasion or variable issue-oriented courses which have the content described in the title. Credit and prerequisites as announced.

    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for a new, advanced, or expanded experience.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • MMED 20406 - Advanced Audio (Music)

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced study of the theories and techniques used in the production of music. Students engage in recording, editing and production of advanced multi-track sessions for music. Topics include the theories of overdubbing, natural sound environments, audio cues and spatial relationship of various sound sources. Lecture and Laboratory.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20329 
    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20407 - Advanced Audio (Post)

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced study of the theories and techniques used in the production of sound for post-production. Students engage in the recording, editing, and production of advanced multi-track sessions for video and motion picture. Topics include the theories of overdubbing, ADR, Foley, natural sound environments, audio cues, and spatial relationship of various sound sources. Lecture and Laboratory.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20329 
    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20408 - Music Business

    Credit Hours: 3
    An examination of the professional music industry. Students will study topics such as music publishing, music licensing, music copyright law and important legal cases, performing rights organizations, royalties, contract negotiations, booking agencies, music distribution and delivery, and artist management.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20409 - Media Business and Entrepreneurship

    Credit Hours: 3
    An examination of the organization, operation and policies of both media organizations and independent media content producers. Students will focus on content evaluation; planning and budgeting; government regulation; and legal, ethical and censorship concerns from the perspective of the media manager/entrepreneur.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced Standing.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20412 - Comparative Mass Media Systems

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of foreign and international mass media systems in terms of structure, financing and content. Topics include the impact of political and cultural institutions on shaping media systems, international telecommunications organizations and mass media and developing nations.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing.
    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20415 - IDM Seminar

    Credit Hours: 1
    A seminar dealing in issues faced by multimedia professionals and preparation for advanced study. Major emphasis will be placed on the development of a professional portfolio, resume writing and interviews for the field of multimedia.

    Prerequisite(s): Junior or senior standing by advisement.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • MMED 20416 - Senior Seminar

    Credit Hours: 1
    Designed to be the capstone experience for mass media and IDM majors, this course examines media trends and practices and further prepares students for advanced study in the media field. Emphasis will be placed on portfolio/tape development and research, writing and presentation skills.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and senior status.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • MMED 20417 - Television Directing

    Credit Hours: 3
    Practice in the planning, casting, rehearsing and production of a television program from the perspective of a television director. Attention will be given to advanced production techniques as well as to the aesthetic and persuasive capabilities of the medium.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and MMED 20330 .
    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20425 - Knacktive

    Credit Hours: 3
    Knacktive replicates the creative demands and intense teamwork atmosphere of a technology-oriented, professional marketing communications agency. Interested students must apply for a specific position on a team, participate in an interview, and be selected by the course faculty before registering.

    Prerequisite(s): Each position on a team has a minimum prerequisite and students can view the list on the Knacktive website, contact a Knacktive faculty member, or contact their respective school office for more information.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20428 - Advertising Campaigns

    Credit Hours: 3
    Combines creative approaches used in creation of advertisements with media planning and buying concepts. Students will concept ad messages as well as choose media strategies and determine media costs. Focus is on how to combine message objectives with media strategies to effectively communicate specific target audiences.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20230 , MMED 20243 , and MMED 20304 .
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20429 - Advanced Problems in Broadcasting-Independent Study

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Research in an area of broadcasting which is of particular interest to the advanced student capable of individual, directed study. Independent studies must be approved by a faculty supervisory committee.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and consent of instructor.
    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for a new, advanced or expanded experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • MMED 20430 - Advanced Advertising Strategies

    Credit Hours: 3
    A capstone course which will apply advertising principles to an actual advertising campaign. Students will form an advertising agency to create a complete advertising campaign, including situational analysis, segment identification, campaign objectives and strategies, budgeting, and creative strategies. Campaign collateral, including print, broadcast and Internet advertisements will be developed by the students and presented to the client.

    Prerequisite(s): Senior Standing and permission of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20450 - Mass Communication Study Abroad

    Credit Hours: 1 to 6
    Courses taken in a foreign country, which complement Mass Media studies. The number of hours assigned is contingent upon course rigor and will be determined by the student’s advisor and school chairperson.

    Repeatable: Course can be repeated for a new, advanced or expanded experience for a total of up to 6 hours.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 6
  

  • MMED 20452 - Internship

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Individual mass communication interests of the student are addressed through practical application through a media outlet appropriate for the student’s area of concentration. Advisement required.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing.
    Repeatable: This course is repeatable if the internship is with a different company or a different position within the previous company.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • MMED 20459 - Independent Study in Journalism

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Research in the area of journalism which is of particular interest to the advanced student capable of individual, directed study. Independent studies must be approved by a faculty supervisory committee; forms are available in the office manager’s office.

    Prerequisite(s): Advanced standing and consent of instructor.
    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for a new, advanced, or expanded experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • MMED 20484 - Digital Cinematography

    Credit Hours: 3
    An in-depth study of the finer points of cinematography. Camera functions, lenses, advanced lighting techniques, contrast principles, advanced picture composition, camera movement and cinematography techniques and philosophies are covered.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20330  or MMED 20417 .
    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20490 - Sound Design and Aesthetics

    Credit Hours: 3
    The culminating course in audio production, Sound Design and Aesthetics examines audio aesthetics and the final stages of audio production through practical experience. Students take on the role of a producer and demonstrate their mastery of recording theories and practices. Topics include advanced mixing, mastering, multi-channel sound design, audio aesthetics and experimental sound.

    Prerequisite(s): MMED 20406  or MMED 20407 .
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MMED 20499 - Field Experience in Mass Media

    Credit Hours: 6 to 12
    This course will offer an opportunity to gain practical experience in the field of mass media. The experience should include a minimum of 300 hours of hands-on supervised practical experience over a 12-16 week period. The experience will be directed and supervised by an approved mass media professional as well as a school supervisor. Field experiences must be approved by a faculty supervisory committee; forms are available in the office manager’s office.

    Prerequisite(s): School consent.
    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for a new, advanced or expanded experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 6 to 12

  • MATH 17120 - Calculus I

    Credit Hours: 4
    An introduction to single-variable calculus. Topics include intuitive treatment of limits and continuity, differentiation of elementary functions, curve sketching, extreme values, areas, rates of change, definite integral and fundamental theorem of calculus. Will satisfy The Northwest Core requirement in mathematics. Proficiency examination is available.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17117  with a minimum grade of C, or MATH 17118  and MATH 17119  with a minimum grade of C in both, or equivalent
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): ACT Math score of at least 27, or a high school GPA of 3.9.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • MATH 17121 - Calculus II

    Credit Hours: 5
    Topics include further applications and integration techniques, an introduction to multivariate topics, and sequences and series.

    Prerequisite(s): A minimum grade of C in MATH 17120  or consent of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 5

  • MATH 17145 - Computational Reasoning and Data

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to the basic techniques of computational mathematics using programming and discipline specific software. The material is presented from a problem-solving perspective with examples from the applied sciences. Topics to be covered include matrix computations, data fitting techniques, data analysis, graphics, and statistics.

    Prerequisite(s): An ACT Math score of 22 or higher, or high school GPA of 3.00 or higher.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17171 - Fundamentals of Mathematics

    Credit Hours: 3
    An analytic exploration of elementary mathematics concepts, including set theory, operations in numeration systems and bases, number theory, operations and applications with rational and real numbers, probability and statistics, logic, relations and modular arithmetic. Will satisfy The Northwest Core requirement in mathematics. Proficiency examination is available.

    Prerequisite(s): An ACT Math score of 22 or higher, high school GPA of 3.00 or higher, or concurrent enrollment in MATH 17071 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17197 - Exploring Mathematics-Related Fields

    Credit Hours: 1
    A course introducing students to what it means to be a mathematics major by experiencing some of the work mathematicians do and the types of careers and opportunities open to mathematics majors beyond teaching.

    Prerequisite(s): A minimum grade of C in MATH 17117  or MATH 17118  or MATH 17120  or MATH 17171 .
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • MATH 17226 - Math in the Elementary Classroom

    Credit Hours: 3
    In this course, candidates will learn strategies and skills for teaching, assessment, and evaluation necessary for instruction of mathematics in the early elementary school. Emphasis will be given to activities which provide experience with various methods of teaching mathematics to early learners, development of a positive student mindset and strategies to evaluate instructional materials.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17171  with a grade of C or better.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17227 - Math in the Elementary School

    Credit Hours: 3
    In this course, candidates will learn strategies and skills necessary for the instruction of Mathematics in the elementary school with an emphasis on developing a positive student mindset, inquiry learning, conceptual understanding, and strategies to evaluate instructional materials. Content will focus on counting, number sense, base ten system, number and operations, measurement, and early fraction understanding.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17171 , EDCI 62108 , EDCI 62116 , and EDCI 62225   (or concurrently) each with a grade of C or better.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17280 - Methods in Teaching with Technology

    Credit Hours: 3
    Introduces technological tools and appropriate methods for using them to teach mathematics and science.

    Prerequisite(s): Successful completion of an Institutional Requirement for Digital Literacy.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17299 - Special Topics

    Will be offered according to student needs and interests. Each offering will be designed to incorporate the latest information pertaining to a timely topic in Mathematics or Statistics.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for additional experience, new material, or progression of study.

  • MATH 17301 - A Bridge to Graduate Mathematics I

    Credit Hours: 3
    This is the first part of a two-course sequence intended for applicants who lack knowledge in undergraduate mathematics needed in the M.S. in Mathematics program.  It includes convergence of sequences and series, partial derivatives, multiple integration, and elementary differential equations.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17120 with minimum grade of B.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17302 - A Bridge to Graduate Mathematics II

    Credit Hours: 3
    This is the second of a two-course sequence intended for applicants who lack knowledge in undergraduate mathematics needed in the M.S. in Mathematics program. This course does not fulfill any requirement for any undergraduate math major or minor.  It includes linear systems, matrix algebra, and methods of proofs.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17120 with a minimum grade of B or consent of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17311 - Linear Algebra

    Credit Hours: 3
    An elementary introduction to finite-dimensional vector spaces and matrices. Topics include linear independence, bases, matrix operations, canonical forms, similarity, invertibility, geometric applications and determinants.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17117  with a minimum grade of C, or MATH 17120  with a minimum grade of C.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17316 - Linear Statistical Models I

    Credit Hours: 3
    Applied course in statistics, including analysis of variance, multiple regression and the use of SAS, a statistical package.

    Prerequisite(s): A minimum grade of C in either MATH 17114  or MATH 17333 , or consent of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17318 - Linear Statistical Models II

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course covering many of the statistical designs and techniques widely used in research and applications.

    Prerequisite(s): A minimum grade of C in either MATH 17114  or MATH 17333 .
    Typically Offered: Odd SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17319 - Graph Theory

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to the theory and applications of graphs. Topics may include undirected and directed graphs and trees.

    Prerequisite(s): A minimum grade of C in MATH 17117  or MATH 17120 .
    Typically Offered: Odd FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • MATH 17321 - Calculus III

    Credit Hours: 4
    Topics include polar and parametric equations, additional multivariate topics, vector valued functions, and vector analysis.

    Prerequisite(s): A minimum grade of C in MATH 17121  or consent of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • MATH 17323 - Math Assessment and Intervention: Theories and Techniques

    Credit Hours: 3
    Candidates in this module will investigate principles of using universal screeners and curriculum-based measures (CBMs) to assess and evaluate student performance in mathematics. Use of assessment tools in mathematics to identify learning problems, to monitor student growth, and to implement response to intervention practices will be explored in this module with content focusing on operations of fractions, ratios and proportions, algebraic thinking, geometry, and number theory.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17227  or MATH 17226 , MATH 17371  each with a C or better and Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Corequisite(s): Elementary and Special Education majors: EDCI 62324  and SCED 28226 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3

  • MATH 17342 - An Introduction to Modeling

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is a bridge between the study of mathematics and applications of mathematics to various fields. The course provides an introduction to the entire modeling process. The course requires extensive use of various software, CAS, Excel spreadsheets, R, or Python, to solve modeling problems. Application areas may include difference equations, model fitting, empirical model construction, divided difference tables, cubic splines, Monte Carlo simulation models, reliability models, linear programming, and nonlinear optimization.

  • MILS 42116 - Foundations of Officership

    Credit Hours: 1
    Introduces the student to issues and competencies central to a commissioned officer’s responsibilities. These initial lessons establish a framework for understanding officership, leadership, and Army values. Addresses life skills including fitness and time management. Designed to give the student an accurate insight into the Army profession and the officer’s role in the Army.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • MILS 42126 - Basic Leadership

    Credit Hours: 1
    Course builds upon the fundamentals introduced in MILS 42116  Foundations of Officership, by focusing on leadership theory and decision making. Life skills lessons include: problem solving, critical thinking, leadership theory, followership, group interaction, goal setting, and feedback mechanisms. Prepares students to advance to more complex leadership instruction concerning the dynamics of organization. Students are increasingly required to demonstrate knowledge of leadership fundamentals and communications.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • MILS 42216 - Individual Leadership Studies

    Credit Hours: 2
    Designed to develop within the student a knowledge of self, self-confidence, and individual skills. Through experiential learning activities, students develop problem-solving and critical-thinking skills, and apply communication, feedback, and conflict-resolution skills. Builds upon the fundamentals introduced in MILS 42116  and MILS 42126 , this course delves into several aspects of communication and leadership theory. Focuses on critical life skills which enable the student’s future success. Includes a major leadership and problem-solving case study which draws upon previous instruction.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • MILS 42226 - Leadership and Teamwork

    Credit Hours: 2
    Course focuses on self-development guided by knowledge of self and group processes. Experiential learning activities are designed to challenge students’ current beliefs, knowledge, and skills. Takes the approach of placing students in a wide variety of group exercises designed to emphasize various leadership competencies and insights. The instructor, acting as facilitator, helps guide student processing of the events to derive the leadership, group dynamics, and problem-solving lessons that the exercises offer. Practical life skills are emphasized.

    Corequisite(s): MILS 42212 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 2
