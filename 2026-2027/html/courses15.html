Physics
  

  • PHYS 25360 - Quantum Mechanics

    Credit Hours: 3
    The philosophy and methods of selected topics from quantum mechanics. Topics include Schrodinger’s equation, simple barrier problems, angular momentum, linear oscillator, hydrogen atom and elementary perturbation.

    Prerequisite(s): PHYS 25350  or CHEM 24532  and CHEM 24533 , MATH 17321  or concurrently.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PHYS 25420 - Advanced Physics Laboratory

    Credit Hours: 1
    Laboratory will be a series of in-depth, investigative activities that will cover a range of principles in Physics.

    Prerequisite(s): PHYS 25230  and PHYS 25231  and MATH 17121 .
    Hours per week: The laboratory will meet at least two hours each week.

    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for additional experience, new material, and progression of study. Maximum of 4 credit hours allowed.

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • PHYS 25430 - Optics

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of modern optics, including reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction, polarization, lasers, holography, non-linear optics, optical detectors and modern applications.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17121 
    Hours per week: Three hours of lecture and discussion each week.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PHYS 25450 - Computational Physics

    Credit Hours: 3
    Introduction to computational methods used to solve problems in physics. Numerical techniques for the analysis of experimental data and the solution of ordinary and partial differential equations will be studied. These numerical methods will be applied to the solution of a variety of problems that arise in classical physics. Visualization and simulation techniques will be studied and the power of these methods to enhance physical understanding will be emphasized.

    Prerequisite(s): PHYS 25230  and PHYS 25231  and CSIS 44141 .
    Typically Offered: Odd SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PHYS 25479 - Undergraduate Research

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    An independent research project done under supervision of a faculty member. The results of the research project are to be summarized in a paper or presentation at the discretion of the faculty member.

    Prerequisite(s): Permission of instructor.
    Repeatable: Can be repeated for new experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • PHYS 25489 - Physics Practicum

    Credit Hours: 1 to 2
    This practicum provides physics majors with experience in using, repairing and building equipment as well as experience in assisting physics faculty in instruction in classes and laboratories. Permission of instructor necessary. Physics minors only.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 2
  

  • PHYS 25499 - Special Investigations in Physics

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Special projects and special experiments in physics which are not included in the regular courses. May be elected successively in one or more hours of credit per semester.

    Repeatable: Can be repeated for new experience. Permission of instructor necessary.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • PHYS 25599 - Selected Advanced Topics

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    Special investigations of experimental or theoretical areas which are not included in the regular courses. May be elected in successive semesters.

    Prerequisite(s): Permission of instructor.
    Repeatable: Can be repeated with different topics.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 4

  • POLS 34210 - Exploring Politics

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an introduction to the study of political science. Students will discover how to study politics in a scientific way. Students will learn how to use various quantitative and qualitative research methods to answer questions about politics.

  • POLS 34215 - Introduction to Political Theory

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides students with an introduction to political thought. The course will discuss the varied conceptions of justice, human nature, rulers, and authority, among many other topics and principles. It will then relate those principles to the political theories and competing ideological approaches that emerge from them.

    Typically Offered: FA

  • POLS 34261 - Introduction to Comparative Politics

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR POSC 202

    This course introduces students to the comparative study of political systems across the contemporary world. Emphasis is placed on understanding similarities and differences among countries, including the United States, by comparing different state systems, regime types, forms of government, and political behaviors. Students are also familiarized with comparative methodology and data analysis relevant to comparative politics.

  • POLS 34312 - The Ethical and Political Challenges of AI

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course explores the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI), ethics, and society, addressing the challenges AI poses to human life and governance. Topics include the ethical uses of AI, the automation of labor, surveillance and privacy, and the potential for AI to alter the landscape of democracy and political power. Students will also grapple with philosophical questions, such as whether AI could ever possess moral standing, what implications AI has for the future of human work, and how AI may reshape long-standing ethical principles.

    Recommended Prerequisite(s): CSIS 44122
    Hours per week: 3

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34321 - State and Local Politics

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an investigation and analysis of the American federal system, including its constitutional, political, and administrative characteristics. An integral portion of the course is a study of the subnational governments - state and local - that help to comprise the federal system.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3

  • POLS 34403 - The World of Political Ideas

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will introduce selected political ideas from the Ancient Greeks to modern times. Ideas covered will represent the development of Western Political Philosophy over time, as well as its comparison with selected Eastern political ideas.

    Prerequisite(s): POLS 34215  or permission of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34405 - American Political Thought

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examines key themes in the development of American Political Thought from the Colonial Era to the present. Among other topics, the course addresses the relationship between religion and politics, rights, revolution, national identity, markets and morals, and the role of America in the world.

    Prerequisite(s): POLS 34215  or permission of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34412 - Race, Ethnicity, and Politics

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an examination of the political status of racial and ethnic minorities in the United States. The focus will be on how race and ethnicity affect rights and access to government, as well as the ways in which it affects political attitudes, participation, and representation. During this course, student will learn how to understand these issues and how to propose policies and solutions based on a scientific approach to questions on race, representation, and racial disparities. The course will encompass a seminar format, with emphasis on reading, discussion, and critical thinking exercises.

    Typically Offered: Odd FA

Credit Hours: 3

  • POLS 34422 - Revolution and Social Movements

    Credit Hours: 3
    The course is a comparative study of the causes and outcomes of world socio-political movements and revolutions affecting governmental change and development since 1500. Emphasis is placed on roles play by elites and masses in specific actions and society’s evolving place in government formation. Cases examined include English, American, French, Latin American, Russian, and Chinese Revolutions, as well as current developments in national regimes.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34424 - Terrorism

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides an overview of terrorism, its societal and technological developments, and its associations with groups and violence. Special attention will be placed on government action and policies concerning prevention and responses to attacks. The course will also examine the politics of terrorism, with a focus on theoretical and empirical studies of its causes and consequences. We will look at both domestic and transnational forms of terrorism with more focus on the latter. We will address the causes of terrorism, considering questions such as when, where, and why terrorism occurs, which individual and which groups resort to terror and why, and who are the targets of terrorists. As we explore these analytical questions, we will also acquire in-depth historical knowledge of a range of groups, from 19th century Anarchists to ISIS and others today.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34435 - Public Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    An emphasis on public policy in terms of personnel and budget administration. This course is divided into two distinct but complimentary parts. The first part of the course will focus on personnel administration in the public sector, while the second part of the course will focus on government budgeting. Case studies and exercises will be used in both parts of the course in order to gain an understanding of the key principles, concepts and methods associated with both of these areas of government.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34436 - Constitutional Law: Powers

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examines the Supreme Court’s role in dealing with basic problems of constitutional law. Topics covered include the powers of the three branches of the national government, federal-state power relations, and Congressional power over commerce.

    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34438 - Constitutional Law: Rights and Liberties

    Credit Hours: 3
    Investigates the legal and political context influencing the exercise of fundamental rights and liberties. It is a study of the lines that must be drawn by a democratic society as it attempts to reconcile individual freedom with the rights of the community. In so doing it examines the way in which the U.S. Constitution, as judicially interpreted, has shaped the American concepts of civil liberties and civil rights.

    Typically Offered: Odd SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34459 - Justice: Theories and Applications

    Credit Hours: 3
    A comparative analysis of theories of justice and they have been applied in the past and are currently affecting criminal justice systems. Special attention will be placed upon examination of restorative justice and the use of the death penalty.

    Prerequisite(s): POLS 34205  OR CRIM 46205   OR POLS 34215 .
    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • POLS 34465 - Substantive Policy

    Credit Hours: 3

A focus on current political issues as related to policy development coupled with the ability to analyze past and future policy options will be emphasized. Topics will vary each semester the course is offered.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for new experiences.

  • POLS 34468 - Political Films in Comparative Perspective

    Credit Hours: 3
    This thematic course explores how film communicates political ideas and represents events related to politics and government. Taking a comparative approach, it examines political themes from multiple country perspectives through a variety of film-based genres, including feature films, documentaries, animation, and television. Topical themes vary each semester and may include relevant comparative topics, such as the development of due process and the legal system, authoritarianism and democracy, human trafficking, and identity politics. The course also considers the political context of the films, and the cinematic techniques filmmakers use to convey political messages.

    Hours per week: 3

    Typically Offered: Even FA, Even SP

Credit Hours: 3

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR PSYC 100

    A survey course designed to introduce students to the foundation of human and animal behavior. Students will study the content and the processes used by behavioral scientists to discover, describe, explain and predict human behavior. The course provides an environment in which students learn how to critically evaluate the biological, social/cultural and psychological variables that contribute to behavior and to reflect on those to develop an informed sense of self and others.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

    Credit Hours: 3
    Designed to allow students to apply the principles learned in general psychology to popular films. Each week, the class will watch one film and participate in a discussion of that film. Films will be chosen to present various models and views of a wide variety of psychological principles; discussion will center on the origins and implications of those views.

  • PSYC 08204 - Introduction to Human Services

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course introduces students to the interdisciplinary approach to the understanding of human services in a multicultural society. Topics include personal/professional characteristics, roles of the human service professional, history of the profession, diverse populations, community resources, client-helper relationship, the helping process, ethical standards, and major theoretical approaches to service delivery. Components of diversity and cultural competence are interwoven within the curriculum and methods of evaluation.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

  • PSYC 08212 - Personal Adjustment

    Credit Hours: 3
    Involves clarification of the assumptions, concepts and problems associated with personal adjustment and the field of mental health. It will include some history, definitions of mental health and mental health models. Emphasis is on preventive mental health, personal adjustment and community-based treatment facilities.

    Typically Offered: SP

  • PSYC 08223 - Abnormal Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    Studies behavior patterns considered by society to be maladaptive. The emphasis is on diagnostic categories, relating diagnostic categories to symptom patterns and studying the etiology of symptom patterns. Therapeutic approaches are also presented.

  • PSYC 08234 - Research Literacy

    Credit Hours: 3
    Information is everywhere, but accurate knowledge can be challenging to find. This class is designed to help students understand research methods and the related principles so they can evaluate information sources, interpret research findings, and draw evidence-based conclusions about a range of topics.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08253 - Theories of Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an in-depth survey of the philosophical and historical foundations of psychology, discussing the influence of important people, ideas, and schools of thought within their social, cultural, political, and economic context. With an emphasis on critically thinking about historical principles and ideologies, the course will include historically engaging experiences as well as philosophical and critical perspectives that led to these approaches and ways of thinking.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08260 - Career Paths and Preparations

Emphasis is placed on preparation for graduate study as well as career options within psychology.

Explores the history of guidance and counseling, the comprehensive guidance program, developmental theory, facilitations models, counseling, large group guidance and guidance curriculum, consultation, coordination, peer facilitator projects, counseling ethics and program accountability.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3

Given that understanding, students will discuss the differences between groups and teams as well as consider the ways in which Psychology can be applied to analyze and improve various aspects of group-based activities.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3

Intended for prospective high school teachers. Attention is given to factors in the home, community and school that influence adolescent behavior and personality.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08299 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • PSYC 08323 - Child and Adolescent Psychopathology

    Credit Hours: 3
    An overview of child and adolescent psychopathology. Provides students with a foundation of knowledge regarding the theoretical explanations of child and adolescent behaviors, as well as the assessment, diagnosis and treatment of these behaviors. Students will gain a better understanding of mental health issues facing children and adolescents.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  or PSYC 08299 .
    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 3

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: SP

Includes exploration of types of relationships, communication patterns and alternative family forms.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3

    The role of the consumer in the marketplace, forces influencing consumer demand, and the broad social and economic implications of consumer behavior.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08332 - Family Resource Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will stimulate students to examine and improve their resource management and clarify the impact of management upon them and their families, upon quality of life, and upon other systems and their environment. The course employs a systems perspective. Students will have opportunities to apply these principles through analyzing management problems of different groups and through a group project.

    Hours per week: 3

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08333 - Developmental Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR PSYC 200

    A comprehensive survey of human developmental principles emerging during conception and continuing through to the process of death. Emphasis is placed upon the physical, intellectual, cognitive and social-emotional growth from research, theoretical and applied viewpoints based upon empirically demonstrated psychological knowledge.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  or PSYC 08299 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/

  • PSYC 08334 - Research Methods I

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to build on concepts and skills covered in PSYC 08234 . It focuses on the logic and design of experimental and non-experimental techniques, ethical considerations in research, internal and external validity, and the role of the scientific method in psychology. Composition of an American Psychological Association (APA) style research proposal is emphasized in this course.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 , PSYC 08234 , and MATH 17114 . Behavioral Science majors must earn a grade of C or higher in each prerequisite course.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08335 - Introduction to Case Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course focuses on the concept, practice, and issues of case management. Students will develop skills in communicating with clients, discerning intercultural issues in practice, and using best practices in documentation. This class will provide students with generalist skills that can be used in a variety of careers.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  or PSYC 08299 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08343 - Biological Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    An examination of the underlying biological mechanisms of behavior, thinking, and emotion. Strong emphasis on introductory neuroanatomy and neurophysiology.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  and PSYC 08234 . Behavioral Science majors must earn a grade of C or higher in each prerequisite course.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08344 - Drugs, Brain, and Behavior

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examination of the relationship between neuronal communication, drug use and behavior. Focuses on a description of basic neuroanatomy, an explanation of nervous system functioning, and how both legal and illicit drugs influence this communication and resulting behavior, thinking and emotion.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 , PSYC 08234  and PSYC 08343 .
    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08345 - Disaster Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    A broad introduction to the field of disaster psychology by examining theory, research and practice in disaster psychology. Focuses on the impact of a disaster on both responders and those directly impacted by the event. Discusses intervention strategies and the role of relief organizations.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  and EDM 36305 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08353 - Psychology of Race and Gender

    Credit Hours: 3
    Explores recent literature and research related to biological and cultural factors influencing the development of racial and gender identities. Explores the ways in which stereotypes and social roles affect our lives at the personal, social and institutional levels.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08360 - Key Issues in Psychology

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Critical examination of status of theory and research involving key issues of contemporary knowledge in psychology.

    Prerequisite(s): Prerequisites as announced.
    Repeatable: Repeatable for topic change.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • PSYC 08363 - Psychology of Personality

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examines individual patterns of behavior and reviews diverse theoretical positions that explain personality in terms of individual dynamics, biology, culture, and cognition. Emphasis is placed on understanding the research and philosophy of personality in the context of widely recognized theories of psychology. A survey of relevant and current research is included.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08365 - Social Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introductory course probing the development of human behavior in its social context. Significant theories and methodologies in social psychology developed within the disciplines of sociology and psychology are considered, along with representative research in areas such as social perception and attribution, interpersonal attraction, aggression, altruism, role- and self-development and group processes.

    Prerequisite(s): SOC 35101  OR PSYC 08103 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08370 - Applied Behavior Analysis

    Credit Hours: 3
    A systematic review of the facts, concepts, and principles which describe classical and operant conditioning, extinction, generalization/discrimination processes, focusing on their application to therapy, self-improvement, education, parenting, and animal training.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: Odd FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08383 - Psychology of Aging

    Credit Hours: 3
    A comprehensive survey of the changes older adults experience in the areas of physical decline, intellectual/cognitive functioning, socioeconomic adjustment and death/dying.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08400 - Special Offering

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • PSYC 08401 - Special Topics in Psychology

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    Each course is designed to address the needs of students and/or to incorporate the latest information pertaining to a timely topic or experience in psychology.

    Repeatable: May be repeated once.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • PSYC 08405 - Parenthood Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course addresses major parenting issues, such as issues and characteristics associated with developmentally appropriate parent-child interaction, as well as parenting roles and responsibilities from a global and intercultural perspective. We will address diverse families and parenting in a variety of cultures, as well as competencies for effective parenting across the lifespan. All of this will be grounded in the context of students’ current and future involvement in parent education, relationships, and experiences. This course embraces knowledge based on scientific discovery (research), as well as preexisting personal knowledge. The course will examine individual culture and self-understanding in relation to the context of parenthood. The development of critical thinking skills is a course goal that exists alongside mastery of course content. Students will have opportunities to develop skills and criteria for evaluating the views and ethno-theories in society concerning parenting and parent-child relationships.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08419 - Independent Study

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Allows an intensive, in-depth study by junior and senior psychology majors and minors in any area of their choice subject to the permission of the professor and the school chairperson. Minimum of 13 hours in psychology and consent of chairperson.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  
    Repeatable: May be repeated for a total of 3 hours for new experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • PSYC 08423 - History and Systems of Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an in-depth survey of the historical foundations of modern psychology, discussing the influence of important people, ideas, and schools of thought within their social, cultural, political, and economic context. With an emphasis on practical application of historical principles and ideals, the course will include historically engaging experiences as well as philosophical and physiological perspectives that led to the approaches and ways of thinking. Taken in the final semester.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  and advanced standing. Requires consent of instructor and division assistant chairperson.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08438 - Principles of Counseling

    Credit Hours: 3
    Provides students with a foundation of information related to the development of professional helpers. Provides tools and ethical guidelines required in the counseling, human services, and other helping professions. Covers career paths of professional helpers, reviews counseling theory, and how active listening is used by the helping professionals.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08443 - Advanced Biological Psychology

    Credit Hours: 3
    An in-depth analysis of the anatomical and physiological correlates of behavior. Emphasis is placed on the physiological and biological basis of sensation and perception, alcohol and drug dependency, neuropsychological disorders, emotion, sleep, and consciousness. Other topics may be included.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103 , PSYC 08234 , PSYC 08334 , and PSYC 08343 .
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08444 - Research Methods II

    Credit Hours: 3
    Builds on concepts from PSYC 08234  and PSYC 08334  through the implementation of a research project. Course objectives include choosing a research design, collecting and analyzing data, and presenting findings to peers. Statistical analyses will also be covered, with an emphasis on cleaning data and testing hypotheses.

    Prerequisite(s): Minimum grade of C in PSYC 08103 , PSYC 08234 , PSYC 08334 , and MATH 17114 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08495 - Field Placement/Internship

    Credit Hours: 3
    The internship is a profession-based learning opportunity designed to provide human services and psychology students with practical, on-the-job work experience that will assist in furthering their career goals. All applicants for internship must have completed at least 20 hours in their respective major and have junior status. Instructor approval required.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for new experience.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08500 - Special Offering

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08103  and consent of instructor and school chairperson.
    Repeatable: Repeatable with topic change.

Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • PSYC 08524 - Independent Living for Special Populations

    Credit Hours: 3
    A lifespan approach to the principles involved in developing independent living skills for those who have disabilities. The course will be designed to provide training and experience in working with the handicapped and the elderly.

    Prerequisite(s): PSYC 08335  or by permission.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PSYC 08526 - Psychology of Leadership

    Credit Hours: 3
    Course is designed for individuals who wish to pursue leadership positions, using research supported psychological principles and theory, in community service, education, business management, and/or the helping professions. Focuses on the application and utilization of basic psychological concepts and principles related to the career area of interest.

    Prerequisite(s): Senior or graduate standing.
    Typically Offered: SU

Credit Hours: 3

Public Health
  

  • PBHL 59223 - Introduction to Public Health

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course offers an introductory experience to the field of public health, providing students with a foundational understanding of key concepts and best practices nationally and globally.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PBHL 59332 - Introduction to Epidemiology

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course introduces students to the fundamental concepts and principles of epidemiology as it is used in the field of public health. Students will explore key concepts of epidemiology as it is related to disease and health outcomes for the population and will be introduced to skills involving critical evaluation of public health data, assessment of the impact of disease on the population, and the role of epidemiology in evidence-based practices.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17114 
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PBHL 59334 - Introduction to Healthcare Systems

    Credit Hours: 3
    This is an introductory course that provides the fundamental components of the healthcare system and its delivery, including the organizational structure, policies, key stakeholders, and ethical considerations. Healthcare systems nationally and globally will be explored on a small and large scale to understand the challenges and opportunities associated with healthcare environments.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PBHL 59336 - Global Public Health

    Credit Hours: 3
    The activities in this course introduce students to different approaches and challenges faced globally in the public health sector by exploring various cultures and how they affect public health outcomes.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PBHL 59340 - Environmental Health

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course explores the relationship of environmental health, both built and natural environment contributing to the health and wellbeing of the population.

    Prerequisite(s): HPER 22114 
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • PBHL 59342 - Public Health Emergency Preparedness

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides students with a comprehensive understanding of public health emergency preparedness, emphasizing practical approaches for managing real-world public health crises. Using foundational principles and case studies, students will explore topics such as disaster response coordination, risk communication, resource management, and ethical considerations in public health emergencies. Through interactive activities and applied learning, students will develop skills necessary for effective preparedness planning and response to natural disasters, pandemics, and other public health threats.

     

    Hours per week: 3

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
