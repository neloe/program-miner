Criminology
  

  • CRIM 46205 - Introduction to Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an introduction to the agencies, institutions, and processes involved in the criminal justice system: law enforcement, the courts, and corrections. It examines the history of these institutions and addresses contemporary social issues relevant to criminal justice in the United States.

    Cross-Listed: POLS 34205 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46210 - Introduction to Criminology

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course introduces the theory and research regarding the causes of criminal behavior. Concepts of crime as well as crime typologies including violent crime, political crime, and economic crimes are explored.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35321 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46215 - Introduction to Corrections

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an introduction to the history, concepts, and realities of the punishment of crime through correctional institutions. It investigates the functions of corrections in the supervision, punishment, and treatment of offenders with an emphasis on the increasing social significance an diversity of the penal system in American society.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35240 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46216 - Probation and Parole

    Credit Hours: 3
    The course examines the practices and procedures of probation and parole in the United States within the adult and juvenile systems.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35215 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46301 - Research Methods in Criminology and Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to the scientific method as it is applied to criminological research. Course content includes a study of basic research issues and strategies utilized by criminological researchers. Basic issues covered include research ethics, causality, the relationship between theory and research methods, and the use of qualitative and quantitative data collection and analysis.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17114 , either CRIM 46210  or SOC 35321  , and CRIM 46310 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46302 - Police and Society

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an introduction to police in American society. The material is organized around the development and structure of the police as an integral part of the criminal justice system. The course addresses issues such as the tension between the coercive power of the police and the expectations of freedom in American society, the ability of the police to deal with crime, the nature and effects of police subculture, issues or profiling, stereotyping, corruption, and policing strategies for improving effectiveness and community relations.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35375 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46303 - Delinquency

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course explores the theories and social contexts of juvenile delinquency. It examines current trends in juvenile delinquency and considers the effectiveness of various programs and policies in response to juvenile delinquency in the United States.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35320 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46305 - Evidence and Investigation

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course examines the techniques of investigation and the processing of evidence by the criminal justice system.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35305 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46310 - Criminological Theory

    Credit Hours: 3
    In this course, students will explore the major and contemporary theories of crime and how they are applied to criminological research, policies, and practices.

    Prerequisite(s): CRIM 46210  or SOC 35321  with grade of C or better.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46315 - Deviant Behavior

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an examination of rule/norm-breaking behavior. It focuses on the nature and meaning of deviance and social control. This course investigates theories of deviance and also types of deviance.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35323 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46317 - Contemporary Issues in Criminology and Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 3
    In this course, students will explore timely and relevant issues within the criminal justice system. Perspectives within criminology will pose the framework for understanding various contemporary issues face by society allowing students to better understand the complexities and challenges of topics presented.

    Prerequisite(s): CRIM 46210  or SOC 35321  and CRIM 46205  or POLS 34205 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46405 - Victimology

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course explores the history of victimology and theories of victimization. It provides an in-depth examination of crime victims, the effects of victimization, experiences of victims within the criminal justice system, and victim advocacy.

    Cross-Listed: SOC 35405 

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • CRIM 46480 - Special Topics in Criminology and Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 1 to 6
    Courses offered on only one occasion or variable issue-oriented courses which have the content described in the title. Credits and prerequisites as announced.

    Repeatable: Course may be repeated for new topics to earn no more than 6 credit hours in total, subject to the approval of the instructor.

Credit Hours: 1 to 6
  

  • CRIM 46490 - Independent Study in Criminology and Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Offered by special arrangement with the consent of the instructor involved and the school chairperson.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for new topics up to 9 credit hours.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • CRIM 46495 - Internship in Criminology and Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 1 to 6
    Provides direct contact with agencies and paraprofessional experiences in the field of criminology and criminal justice. Students will apply knowledge from criminology and criminal justice courses. Credit hours will vary according to the nature and duration of the internship.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for up to 6 credit hours of Criminology and Criminal Justice electives.

Credit Hours: 1 to 6
  

  • CRIM 46499 - Research Seminar in Criminology and Criminal Justice

    Credit Hours: 3
    An intensive seminar in issues pertinent to the emphases within the major purposed to produce a research paper or project and class presentation of the student’s choice in consultation with the instructor. Students should demonstrate knowledge and skills acquired in the degree program within the final paper/project, including appropriate methodological application and statistical analysis.

    Prerequisite(s): CRIM 46301 , and CRIM 46205  or POLS 34205 , and CRIM 46210  or SOC 35321 .
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): Completion of 39 hours within the degree program prior to taking the course.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

  • CSIS 44356 - Network Fundamentals

    Credit Hours: 3
    Course introduces the architecture, structure, functions, components, and models of the Internet and other computer networks. The Open Systems Interconnection (OSI) and Transport Control Protocol (TCP) layered models are used to examine the nature and roles of protocols and services at the application, network, data link, and physical layers. The principles and structure of IP addressing and the fundamentals of Ethernet concepts, media and operations are introduced.

    Prerequisite(s): Prerequisite for undergraduate students: CSIS 44130  or CSIS 44101  with a grade of C or better. Prerequisite for graduate students: CSIS 44542  with a grade of B or better, or concurrent enrollment in CSIS 44542 , or consent of instructor.

  • CSIS 44422 - Generative AI for Technologists

    Credit Hours: 3

Students will gain practical experience in developing AI-based chatbots, leveraging tools such as graph databases and vector databases for retrieval-augmented generation. The course also emphasizes prompt engineering, security risks, privacy concerns, and ethical considerations, with a final project that allows students to apply these skills in real-world applications.

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to the design of data-intensive, reliable, scalable, and maintainable systems.   • CSIS 44520 - Web Mining

    Credit Hours: 3
    The Web, the largest publicly accessible data source in the world, has grown at an unprecedented pace during the past decade. Rapidly changing news and events make the Internet a non-stationary environment which, in turn, generates rapidly changing data. This course examines the unique characteristics of the Web from a data mining perspective. It provides a review of data mining foundations and covers basic concepts of text mining, information retrieval, web search and data mining applications on social media.

    Prerequisite(s): Undergraduate prerequisite: A grade of C or better in CSIS 44242 . Graduate prerequisite: A grade of C or better in CSIS 44642.
