Economics
  

  • ECON 52354 - Labor Economics

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of economic theory and evidence related to labor supply and demand; the wage structure within the economy; labor’s share of national income; unemployment, human resource development, poverty and labor market discrimination; the economic effects of unions; and other issues related to the employment of human resources and labor-management relations.

    Prerequisite(s): ECON 52150  and ECON 52151 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ECON 52361 - Sports Economics

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course investigates questions relating to the contribution of professional and recreational sports to social welfare. The general objective of the course is to apply economic concepts and ideas to the sports industry.

    Prerequisite(s): ECON 52151 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ECON 52362 - Comparative Economic Systems

    Credit Hours: 3
    How market, mixed, planned, and developing economic systems are organized and function to obtain major economic, political and social goals.

    Prerequisite(s): ECON 52150  and POLS 34102 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ECON 52425 - Internship in Economics

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Economics majors may, with approval from the School Chairperson, enroll in an internship section. This is recommended for students with junior or senior standing.

    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for new experience.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • ECON 52450 - International Economics

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of modern theories in international trade and finance and current trends and issues in international economic relations.

    Prerequisite(s): ECON 52150  and ECON 52151 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ECON 52451 - Econometrics

    Credit Hours: 3
    Foundations of econometrics. Analysis of linear regression, non-linear regression and computer simulation models. The problems of parameter estimation, verification and the prediction of economic magnitudes are studied.

    Prerequisite(s): ECON 52150  and ECON 52151 , MATH 17114 .
    Typically Offered: Odd SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ECON 52458 - Forecasting Business Conditions

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of applied economics encompassing measurement, analysis and prediction of business conditions at the national, regional and industrial levels.

    Prerequisite(s): ECON 52150 , ECON 52151  and MATH 17114  and MATH 17118 .
    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ECON 52459 - Independent Study in Economics

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Economics majors may, with approval from the School Chairperson, enroll in an independent study section. The approval must be received during the term preceding enrollment.

    Repeatable: This course is repeatable with topics changing.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3

Education: Curriculum & Instruction
  

  • EDCI 62107 - Ecology and Developmental Foundations of Education

    Credit Hours: 2
    Beginning education students are introduced to the systemic relationships in which they develop as social and cultural beings. Through intentionally designed field experiences, students are oriented to the field of education and guided to examine the direct and indirect influences that impact teaching and learning. These influences include but are not limited to immediate family, interactions between school, home and community, federal and regional mandates and laws, as well as cultural and generational beliefs. Through intentionally designed field work and guided discussion, students will learn to interpret their observations and ‘noticings’ from the lens of developmental and learning theories. The course includes 10 hours of field experiences.

    Corequisite(s): EDCI 62113 
Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62108 - Introduction to Curriculum, Instruction and Assessment

    Credit Hours: 2
    This course builds on the experiences of students and focuses on the development of curriculum and instruction based on the unique needs of individual students and culturally responsive pedagogy.  Candidates will be introduced to national and state standards that frame curriculum development while at the same time realizing that instruction is driven by the analysis of student assessment results. Candidates will examine the foundation of assessment planning as well as the tools necessary to use assessment as the impetus for developing instruction that meets the needs of all learners.  This course requires 10 hours of field experience work. 

    Corequisite(s): EDCI 62116 
Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62109 - Positive Communication in an Inclusive Classroom

    Credit Hours: 2
    This course will provide candidates with knowledge and understanding about collaborating with various stakeholders to create positive inclusive classrooms to foster student engagement, to meet the needs of diverse learners, to implement multiple teaching/learning strategies, to utilize accommodations/modifications, and to effectively manage a classroom.  This course provides candidates with knowledge and understanding about the history of special education and inclusion, sources of referral to parents and teachers, identification procedures for individuals with disabilities (including the impact of language, culture, environment and mental health issues), inclusive methods for teaching students with disabilities, those with gifts and talents, and English language learners.  Candidates will be introduced to factors to consider when developing an inclusive and positive classroom environment such as emotional intelligence, strengths-based teaching, and preventative strategies. This course will offer an introduction to communication and its importance in teaching and learning, including verbal and non-verbal, media assisted, and socio-cultural difference. The course requires 10 hours of field experience.

    Corequisite(s): EDCI 62119  
Credit Hours: 2

Emphasis is placed on facilitating professional growth by acknowledging and reinforcing the importance of developmental foundations in educational goal-setting, developing an awareness of bias and assumptions in interpreting data, and the need to engage in culturally responsive practice.   • EDCI 62222 - Social Studies in the Elementary School

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides students with an introduction to organizational and curricular issues in teaching social studies in elementary grades. It focuses on understanding the interrelated nature of the key social studies disciplines of history, economics, government, sociology, and geography. Key concepts in social studies education will be addressed such as curriculum standards and content knowledge expectations. Candidates will learn standards-based content, trends and issues in social studies education, inquiry-based pedagogies, discipline-specific thinking skills in the social studies, teaching and learning resources, and effective teaching practices all aimed at developing the knowledge and skills students need (such as critical thinking and problem solving) for full participation in global and diverse society.

  • EDCI 62302 - Early Childhood Developmental Foundations

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course allows candidates to reflect on the meaning of early childhood and provides an in-depth exploration of the development of the whole child, focusing on the interconnected domains of physical, cognitive, social-emotional, and language development from birth - age eight. Candidates will examine theories of development and reflect on biological, educational, environmental, cultural, and familial influences that contribute to children’s growth and learning. Candidates will examine the critical role movement has in young children’s learning and development. Through hands-on experiences, candidates will learn to design and implement movement-based learning opportunities that foster coordination, creativity, problem-solving, self-regulation, and brain development and recognize movement as a powerful pathway to learning. Through classroom observation and analysis, candidates will develop an individual pedagogy that reflects their personal teaching philosophy.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62303 - Early Childhood Assessment

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course intends to assist teacher candidates to develop an understanding of assessment in early childhood education (Birth-8) and identify the best strategies to assess children’s learning and development. Some main topics included in the course are assessment and evaluation, progress monitoring, screening, and using the assessment data to support children’s learning and development.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62304 - Practicum: Early Learning

    Credit Hours: 3
    Candidates will participate in supervised field experiences with toddlers, two-year-olds, and preschool children in a Reggio Emilia classroom environment. The course explores the Reggio Emilia philosophy with a focus on the provocation process as a framework for designing developmentally appropriate learning experiences. Candidates will strengthen their understanding of the importance of using observation, documentation, and reflection to plan and implement intentional provocations which spark inquiry, creativity, and critical thinking in young children. The course introduces candidates to Conscious Discipline, an evidence-based approach to classroom management and social-emotional learning. Through practical application, candidates will explore strategies and understand the adults’ role in supporting children’s social and emotional growth. Candidates will reflect on professional dispositions, meet regularly with cooperating teachers and professor to set personal goals for the course. 

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62312 - Developing Foundations of Early Childhood Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to equip the educator with relevant child development knowledge that impacts curricular and behavioral decisions in the classroom, so as to be able to engage in evidence-based practices. Students will outline typical developmental milestones and identify red flags from birth to age 12; explore a variety of contextual and maturational factors that guide, support, and alter the ongoing development of children in the physical, socio-emotional, cognitive and language domains; evaluate the influence of developmental factors on current educational issues; and formulate a child development based philosophical and theoretical framework for their work with children. Observation and participation with children at the child development center and cooperating schools will be an integral part of the course.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3

  • EDCI 62346 - Introduction to Trauma-Informed Practices

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will provide students with an introductory overview of the concept of trauma including terminology, key concepts, types of trauma and adversities, causes, and short-and long-term consequences of trauma. Students will increase their knowledge on how trauma significantly impacts academic performance, developmental domains, behavior, and social interactions.

    Hours per week: 3

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62353 - Teaching Reading in Content Areas

    Credit Hours: 2
    Topics will focus on basic reading skills as relevant to all academic areas in the junior and senior high schools, with additional attention to specialized skills of reading as related to particular content materials.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62356 - Teaching Reading/Language Arts in the Elementary School

    Credit Hours: 4
    This course has been designed to provide methods of instruction for an integrated elementary/middle school reading and language arts program. Emphasis will be placed on methods, materials and teaching approaches which will assist children in developing the lifelong communication skills of listening, speaking, reading and writing.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • EDCI 62371 - Introduction to Special Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    Provides participants with knowledge and understanding about the identification of exceptional children, methods of working with exceptional children and sources of referral available to parents and teachers.

    Typically Offered: F, SS

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62372 - Systems for Supporting Instruction and Behavior for Students with Disabilities

    Credit Hours: 2
    This course is designed to introduce candidates to the issues, characteristics, legal aspects, professional, and ethical considerations of teaching students with mild/moderate disabilities from a cross categorical perspective. This course will provide candidates with the understanding of how disabilities may interact with development and learning and use this knowledge to provide differentiated, meaningful, and challenging learning experiences and curricula for individuals with disabilities.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62371 
Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62380 - Diagnostic and Corrective Reading

    Credit Hours: 2
    Diagnostic techniques and procedures for the correction of learning problems in reading.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62301  and EDCI 62356 , and Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62382 - Introduction to Cross Categorical Special Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    Designed to develop knowledge of the issues and characteristics of individuals with mild/moderate disabilities (e.g., emotional and intellectual disabilities, learning disabilities, behavior disorders and/or physical and other health impaired, including ADHD) and to provide information and organizational techniques to meet their educational needs in a cross categorical setting. Students will develop the conceptual frameworks needed to identify and interpret a broad range of student behavior and deficits from a cross-categorical perspective.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62371 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62384 - Teaching Students in Cross Categorical Special Education: Academic/Behavioral

    Credit Hours: 3

Credit Hours: 3

    Credit Hours: 3
    Designed to equip teachers-in-training with the knowledge and skills to effectively teach adolescents and young adults with mild/moderate academic and/or behavioral disabilities. This is one of two methods courses (with no required sequence) required for the major in Special Education: Cross Categorical. Permission of advisor/instructor needed to enroll.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62371  and EDCI 62382 , and Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA

This is one of two methods courses (with no required sequence) required for the major in Special Education: Cross Categorical.

This is one of two methods courses (with no required sequence) required for the major in Special Education: Cross Categorical. Permission of instructor needed to enroll.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62371  and EDCI 62382 , and Admittance to Teacher Education.

  • EDCI 62394 - Special Education: Theory and Techniques of Evaluation, Instruction, and Behavior

    Credit Hours: 4
    This course will provide candidates with knowledge and understanding of assessment, evaluation, curriculum, and instruction for students with mild/moderate disabilities including academic, behavioral, functional, and physical performance. Candidates will be introduced to individual, family, school, and community factors to consider when collaborating to develop culturally responsive learning environments, assessments, curricula, instructional, and behavioral strategies from a cross-categorical perspective.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62372 
Credit Hours: 4
  

  • EDCI 62396 - Special Education: Implementing Techniques and Strategies of Evaluation, Instruction, and Behavior

    Credit Hours: 4
    This course will provide candidates with development and implementation of assessment, evaluation, curriculum, and instruction for students with mild/moderate disabilities including academic, behavioral, functional, and physical performance. Candidates will collaborate to plan, develop, implement, and analyze culturally responsive learning environments, assessments, curricula, instructional, and behavioral strategies from a cross-categorical perspective.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62394  with a grade of C or better and admittance to Teacher Education.
Credit Hours: 4

    Credit Hours: 2
    Supervised practice in culturally responsive teaching with preschool children in group settings.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.

    Supervised practice in culturally responsive teaching with Kindergarten children in group settings.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.

    Credit Hours: 1
    Supervised practice in teaching language arts.

  • EDCI 62408 - Transition and Career Readiness, Families, and Partnerships: Theory and Techniques

    Credit Hours: 2
    This course will provide candidates an opportunity to experience the transition process through the development of a coordinated set of activities designed for students with disabilities through an outcome-oriented process, involving collaboration and family partnerships that promotes movement from school to post-school activities. Candidates will become familiar with the principles, methods, and procedures of career readiness, career education, and transition for students with disabilities.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62396  with a grade of C or better and admittance to Teacher Education.

  • EDCI 62410 - Elementary School Social Studies

  • EDCI 62420 - Content Teaching in Elementary Schools

    Credit Hours: 3
    Preservice teachers will develop knowledge in the content areas and teach content area subjects (math, science and social studies) under the guidance and supervision of a faculty member and a cooperating teacher in a classroom setting.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62320 , EDCI 62410 , SCED 28380  and MATH 17471 , and Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

  • EDCI 62421 - Early Childhood Curricular Approaches

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will emphasize and inform candidates of the importance of play-based and process-oriented learning approaches, and developmentally appropriate practice in Early Childhood settings. Candidates will explore the Constructivist philosophy - pedagogy and conceptual framework, including underlying theories and contemporary influences in early childhood education. The course will focus on key elements of Constructivism - collaboration, role of the teacher as a researcher, learner autonomy, critical thinking and problem solving, and active engagement will be addressed. Candidates will examine notable Constructivist theorists, trends, theories influencing child development, and curriculum models (Waldorf, Montessori, Reggio Emilia, Bank Street, High Scope, Creative Curriculum, and Project Construct) that shape current evidence-based early childhood education programs. Candidates will learn to plan developmentally appropriate, meaningful learning experiences based on state standards and early learning goals with an understanding of how content areas are integrated to support the whole child. Candidates will engage in environmental organization and design that encourage and nurture active exploration and curiosity in an early childhood setting.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62426 - Curriculum and Assessment Grades K-3

    Credit Hours: 3
    Candidates will develop a comprehensive knowledge of curriculum, systematic instruction, and assessment specific to kindergarten through third grade. Emphasis is placed on designing and implementing integrated, developmentally appropriate learning experiences that promote meaningful connections across content areas. Candidates will explore and apply principles of differentiated learning and instruction to meet the diverse needs, strengths, and interests of all children in inclusive classroom settings. State learning standards will be utilized in the development, design, analysis, and implementation of instructional plans. Candidates will design, analyze, and interpret assessment data, with a particular focus on literacy-based assessment and intervention, to make informed instructional decisions that support each child’s growth and learning. The course integrates music, movement, art, and drama as essential modalities for expression, engagement, and learning, encouraging candidates to create dynamic, culturally responsive, and creative classroom environments that nurture the whole child.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62430 - Teaching Writing in Middle School

    Credit Hours: 2
    A study of the methods and materials for teaching the process of writing to middle school students. Content includes: developmental stages of writing, the writing process, writing evaluation, writing across the curriculum, creating a stimulating writing environment and the use of technology in writing.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62431 - Residency Practicum

    Credit Hours: 1 to 8
    Candidates in this course will synthesize the information learned throughout Phase I, II, and III of the professional education program as well as the information learned in general education classes. Candidates will spend the entire semester in a school building, implementing all aspects of curriculum, instruction, assessment, classroom management, and differentiating for special needs populations. Both on-site teaching will be conducted as well as in-class instruction on methodologies.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
Credit Hours: 1 to 8
  

  • EDCI 62432 - Professional Capstone

    Credit Hours: 3
    Taken concurrently with EDCI 62431 , the professional capstone will allow candidates to reflect on their practicum experiences through the lens of implementation of curriculum, instruction, assessment, classroom management, and differentiating for special needs populations. Candidates will meet in cohorts to learn from school personnel concerning their interpretation of effective schools and teachers, multicultural needs, career opportunities, and organizational patterns.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62325 
    Corequisite(s): Elementary and Special Education majors: EDCI 62431  and EDCI 62433 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62433 - Professional Seminar

    Professional Seminar will bring together teacher candidates to share and reflect upon the knowledge, skills, and dispositions gained in EDCI 62431  applied to their classroom contexts. By working in small groups of colleagues and peers, learning will be emergent, targeted, and collaborative. Successful completion of EDCI 62433  will be contingent upon successful completion of EDCI 62431 .

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62324 
    Corequisite(s): Elementary and Special Education majors: EDCI 62431 .
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • EDCI 62434 - Professional Capstone

    Credit Hours: 1
    Professional Capstone will bring together teacher candidates to share and reflect upon the knowledge, skills, and dispositions gained in EDUC 61471  applied to their classroom contexts. By working in small groups of colleagues and peers, emergent learning will be transparent, targeted, and collaborative. Successful completion of EDCI 62434  will be contingent upon successful completion of EDUC 61471 .

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62433 
    Corequisite(s): Elementary and Special Education majors: EDUC 61471 .
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • EDCI 62435 - Practicum: Primary Classrooms

    Credit Hours: 3
    Practicum: Primary Classrooms is designed to enable students to apply theoretical knowledge of pedagogical principles and developmentally appropriate practices in a primary classroom. This integrated experience is supported by information gained through co-requisite coursework focusing on integrated curriculum planning, methods, strategies, assessment, and an understanding of whole child development. Candidates will develop and emerge with a unique professional style of teaching, and strengthen their practice through a continuous cycle of applying what they learn through coursework, reflecting on what they do and how children respond, as well as grow professionally to be reflective practitioners. Candidates will also engage in reflection and application of classroom management theories, considering how different theoretical perspectives inform interactions, routines, and expectations in primary learning settings. Building on these frameworks, the course integrates Positive Behavior Supports (PBS) as a proactive, relationship-based approach to fostering children’s social-emotional competence and creating inclusive classroom communities.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62441 - Early Childhood Leadership and Program Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides teacher candidates with foundational and advanced knowledge of the legal and ethical aspects of early childhood education, including resumes and interviewing, regulations, accreditation standards, program development, and evaluation including educator evaluation. Emphasis is placed on leadership responsibilities, professional ethics, special education considerations, and effective classroom and behavior management practices integrating social and emotional learning. Through critical inquiry and applied learning, candidates will explore how early childhood curriculum philosophies and behavioral guidance approaches align with policies and procedures essential to operating and managing high-quality early childhood programs. Additionally, students will engage in research-based inquiry to strengthen their understanding of educational leadership and program improvement.

    Typically Offered: FA, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62442 - Early Childhood Professional Capstone

    Credit Hours: 3
    Through this culminating course candidates re-visit content in special education, classroom and behavior management, school law, and profession related topics in preparation for student teaching. Through a synthesis of previous coursework, opportunity to learn from school personnel and administrators, and a capstone practicum experience in a diverse setting, this course is designed to develop competency in functioning as an efficient and knowledgeable member in the field of education.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62443 - Early Childhood Professional Learning Community VII

    Credit Hours: 1
    Professional learning communities will bring together candidates to share and reflect upon the knowledge, skills and dispositions gained in EDCI 62442 ; EDCI 62441 ; EDCI 62472  or EDUC 61480  applied to their classroom contexts. By working in small groups of colleagues and peers, learning will be emergent, targeted, and collaborative.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • EDCI 62444 - Practicum in Special Education: Cross Categorical

    Credit Hours: 3
    Supervised clinical experience that includes interpretation of intelligence tests, formal and informal diagnostic procedures, instruction and practical application of teaching strategies and behavior management techniques.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62384  or EDCI 62385 , EDCI 62386  or EDCI 62387 , EDCI 62470  and EDCI 62471 , or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62445 - Student Teaching Seminar

    Student Teaching Seminar will bring together teacher candidates to share and reflect upon the knowledge, skills and dispositions gained in EDUC 61473  applied to their classroom contexts. By working in small groups of colleagues and peers, learning will be emergent, targeted and collaborative.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Corequisite(s): EDUC 61473  
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • EDCI 62446 - Trauma-Informed Practices and Culturally Responsive Pedagogy

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to emphasize and inform candidates on the importance of understanding trauma and the prevalence of trauma in the school system. Candidates will gain knowledge of adverse childhood experiences and understand the negative impact of trauma on development and behavior, specifically brain development. This course explores the intersection of executive functioning, self-regulation, and coping processes in individuals impacted by trauma. Candidates will examine trauma through the lens of epigenetics and generational trauma. This course will inform candidates on how to identify appropriate strategies to support young children’s social-emotional learning. Candidates will identify and evaluate community resources and multidisciplinary supports for children and families affected by trauma.  

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62447 - Methods in Early Childhood Special Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to develop skills, which enable to prospective teacher of the young child with special needs (birth to age 8 years), to function as a facilitator of life-long learning in a world of change. The teachers-in-training will develop the knowledge to plan and organize appropriate curriculum content and learning sequences, identify and implement appropriate instructional strategies, techniques, and environments, and work effectively with parents and families, caregivers, and other professionals.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62454 - Special Education: Residency Practicum

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will provide candidates an opportunity to experience many of the various professional responsibilities of a teacher in special education. The course provides a supervised clinical field experience designed to bridge the gap between theory and practice. It is a problem-solving process; a time for trying one’s self in the role of a teacher while gaining self-knowledge and technical skills that includes interpretation of intelligence tests, formal and informal evaluation/assessment procedures, evidence-based instruction, and practical application of teaching strategies and behavior management techniques.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62408  with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62455 - Family and Community Resources in Early Childhood

    Credit Hours: 2
    This course is a study of family and community resources used in working with children birth through grade 3 and their families with an aim to empower parents as first educators of children. Candidates are introduced to family systems theories, family involvement research, and family development dynamics within pluralistic cultures to help them engage in relationship-based practice as teachers of young children.

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62456 - Practicum and Seminar in Elementary School Reading

    Credit Hours: 3
    Supervised practice in teaching reading with weekly reflections regarding methods and strategies of teaching reading.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62470 - Assessment in Special Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    Instruction in administration and interpretation of formal and informal assessment instruments, interpretation of individualized intelligence tests and instruction in authentic assessment measures in order to design appropriate intervention programs for students with special instructional needs.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62301  and EDCI 62371 , and Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Corequisite(s): EDCI 62471 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62471 - Assessment in Special Education Lab

    Credit Hours: 1
    Students will have the opportunity to administer assessments and develop evaluation plans, IEPs and other documents within the special education process. In addition, diagnosing student needs and prescribing appropriate educational interventions will be emphasized.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Corequisite(s): EDCI 62470 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • EDCI 62472 - Capstone Practicum Experience

    Credit Hours: 2
    This course provides a culminating, supervised pre-student teaching experience that allows teacher candidates to apply their knowledge and skills in an early childhood or elementary classroom setting. Under the guidance of a qualified cooperating teacher, candidates will engage in planning, instruction, assessment, including routines and procedures and classroom management. Students will become familiar with the educator evaluation system standards and artifacts.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62503 - Special Topics in Education

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    This course will provide opportunities for students to study a selected topic or emerging issue in education.

    Repeatable: The course is repeatable with topic change for a total of four hours.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • EDCI 62504 - Creating Inclusive Classrooms

    Credit Hours: 3
    Designed to provide a framework for creating learning environments that support the learning and socialization of students who bring unique characteristics to the classroom. Teachers will develop effective techniques and methods for all students including: those who are at risk for success or who have disabilities; the gifted and talented; those whose diversity lies in their culture, language, race, gender, sexual orientation or family structure.

    Prerequisite(s): Undergraduate admitted to Teacher Education and at least one special education methods course (EDCI 62384  or EDCI 62386 ); graduate Degree Audit Form on file or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
