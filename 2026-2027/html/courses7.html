Education: Curriculum & Instruction
  

  • EDCI 62507 - Trauma-Informed Organizations

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course addresses aspects of how to plan, organize, and administer trauma-informed practices across an organization. Trauma’s impact on youth at school and in other structured settings, building trauma-informed frameworks, and implementing organization wide trauma-informed strategies and approaches is the focus.

    Prerequisite(s): Junior, Senior, or Graduate student status is required.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62508 - Transition/Career Education for Students with Disabilities

    Credit Hours: 2
    Acquaints the student with an understanding of principles, methods, and procedures of career development and transition for students with disabilities.

    Prerequisite(s): Undergraduate admitted to Teacher Education and at least one special education methods course (EDCI 62384  or EDCI 62386 ); graduate EDCI 62642 or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: SP, SU

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDCI 62511 - Middle School Curriculum, Methods and Practicum

    Credit Hours: 3
    Methods and techniques of teaching, practicum experience, and curriculum construction for the middle school, with attention given to early adolescent development.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, Even SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62519 - Problems in Elementary Education

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    A course designed to meet the individual student’s educational needs. The studies may be either of a subjective or objective research nature.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • EDCI 62520 - Introduction to Teaching Students with Autism Spectrum Disorders

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is intended to develop effective teachers of students with autism spectrum disorders. This course will cover a wide variety of topics regarding students with autism spectrum disorders: from characteristics, basic knowledge of assessment, to effective practices used to address their unique needs in school, family, and community. This course will provide an introduction and overview of issues surrounding the education of students with autism spectrum disorders.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62521 - Teaching Students with Autism Spectrum Disorders

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is intended to develop effective teachers of students with autism spectrum disorders (ASD). This course is a follow-up to the prerequisite Introduction to Teaching Students with Autism Spectrum Disorders  and will go into more depth with methods and evidence-based practices used with students with ASD. Students will gain more specific knowledge of strategies and evidence-based practices as well as develop some supports to be used with students in special education or inclusion settings.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62520  
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62522 - Technology for Teaching and Learning

    Credit Hours: 3
    Students will learn about various technologies, applications, procedures and assessments to facilitate student learning. Using technology to meet the diverse needs of students including those with disabilities will be explored. Topics include assessment, levels of assistive technology devices and services, use of word processing, spreadsheet, and database software in the instructional process, student and teacher productivity applications, ethical and legal issues regarding technology in the classroom, and instructional software.

    Prerequisite(s): Prerequisite for undergraduate students: Admittance to Teacher Education and at least one methods course or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62531 - Curriculum and Methods in Early Childhood

    Credit Hours: 3
    A gradual induction into planning, creating and integrating developmentally appropriate curriculum and materials for working with children birth through kindergarten.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62340  and Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62540 - Behavior Management Techniques

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to provide teachers with both technical and functional understanding of applied behavior analysis, as well as strategies for the everyday applications of behavior management in classrooms and other educational settings.

    Prerequisite(s): Undergraduate Admittance to Teacher Education and at least one special education methods course (EDCI 62384  or EDCI 62386 ); graduate Degree Audit form on file or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: F, SS

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62541 - Assessment and Early Intervention in Early Childhood

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to enable pre-service teachers or graduate students to develop a knowledge base of assessment principles so they are able to appropriately assess children’s developmental characteristics and implement intervention strategies to maximize development in young children.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62340  and EDCI 62531 , and Admittance to Teacher Education, or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: F, SS

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62542 - Curriculum and Methods in Early Childhood Special Education

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to develop skills to enable the prospective teacher of the young child with special needs (birth to age five years) to: (1) plan and organize appropriate curriculum content and learning sequences; (2) identify and implement appropriate instructional strategies, techniques, and environments; (3) work effectively with parents and families, as well as other professionals and caregivers, and (4) become knowledgeable about the elements of Individualized Educational Plans (IEPs) and processes including the legal and ethical aspects.

    Prerequisite(s): EDCI 62426  and Admittance to Teacher Education.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62552 - Language, Speech, and Hearing

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will acquaint the student with the development, characteristics, and disorders of language, speech, and hearing in the exceptional individual. The focus is on establishing interactions that are supportive of communicatively handicapped individuals in special education programs.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62553 - The Reading Program in the Secondary School

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course for specialized personnel in reading at the secondary level. Topics will focus on means for identifying students’ basic and developmental skill needs, as well as materials and techniques for instruction.

    Typically Offered: SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62555 - Trends in Reading

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    This course is designed to update teachers in methods, techniques and innovations in teaching reading.

    Repeatable: Repeatable with change in topic.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • EDCI 62563 - Trends in Special Education

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    This course is designed to update teachers in methods, techniques and innovations in special education.

    Repeatable: Repeatable with a change in topic.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • EDCI 62574 - Teaching Math to Students with Disabilities

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will provide an in-depth look at methods used for teaching math through observation and practice to students with math deficits/disabilities. Teacher candidates will learn how to identify students with deficits/disabilities in the areas of math and understand their unique needs, select, use, and implement evidence-based strategies and instructional interventions for students with math deficits/disabilities, analyze standards to create learning objectives, select content and resources, conceptualize a unit, prepare and implement a lesson plan with the effective use of technology, devise and evaluate assessments, and use data to evaluate student progress.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62580 - Methods and Techniques for Teaching English Language Learners (ELL/ESOL)

    Credit Hours: 3
    Designed to introduce the student to the current methods and techniques used to instruct limited English proficient students regardless of their age group or goal. Special attention is given to application of the Missouri WIDA and TESOL Standards to the development of curriculum of the content of ELL class. Explores how current trends in developing second language competence is used to teach language in ESL and mainstream classrooms through a content-based approach. Required for an ESL endorsement.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62582 - Linguistics for Teaching English Language Learners (ELL/ESOL)

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examines the nature of language through a study of pragmatics, semantics, syntax, morphology and phonology. Explores language variation as it is shaped by society and culture. The concepts of first and second language acquisition, the neurological foundations of language, and the evolution of writing systems are introduced.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDCI 62591 - Seminar in Education

    Credit Hours: 1
    Current Trends and Issues Seminar is a required component of the Professional Semester experience and is taken in conjunction with the student teaching experience. It is designed to help students become reflective teachers by providing an opportunity to synthesize the student teaching experience and by providing a forum for collaborative, critical inquiry.

    Corequisite(s): EDUC 61471 , EDUC 61473 , and/or EDUC 61474 .
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1

Emergency and Disaster Management
  

  • EDM 36305 - Disaster Response and Recovery

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides a broad introduction to the field of emergency management. It focuses on the different types of hazards, the individuals and agencies involved in response and recovery operations, human behaviors in disaster situations, hazard detection, warning, evacuation and sheltering, care for the injured, dead, and distressed, management of media relations, donations and volunteers, methods for facilitating recovery and mitigation, and the role of technology.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36306 - Principles of Humanitarian Relief

    Credit Hours: 3
    An examination of the principles, practitioners, and practices of humanitarianism since 1863 with special emphasis on the United States. This course provides students with historical, theoretical, and practical understanding of humanitarianism in the modern world.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP, Odd Years

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36307 - Business Continuity

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to familiarize students to the way businesses prepare for and respond to hazard events. As the number of disasters increase worldwide, businesses must mitigate against losses and ensure continuity of operations. The course will discuss hazard analysis, risk management of operations planning, crisis communications and leadership, and other strategies to minimize impacts of a harmful event.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36308 - Emergency Management Planning

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides an understanding of the emergency management planning process within government organizations, businesses, and non-profits. This course will take students through the steps required and address the best practices currently being utilized in the field.

    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36309 - Vulnerability and Mitigation

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course studies how society is vulnerable to various hazard events at the individual, community, and societal level. Focus will be given to vulnerability theory, and current approaches to mitigate the impacts of hazard events.

    Typically Offered: Odd FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36310 - Nonprofit Organizations

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to provide students with an awareness of basic nonprofit operations. Topics covered include but are not limited to history of the nonprofit sector, project management, volunteer management, organizational leadership, grant writing, and event management.

    Typically Offered: Odd SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36311 - Fire Academy I

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course meets state requirements for Firefighter I and II, First Responder, and Hazardous Material as recognized by the Missouri Division of Fire Safety. This course in organized to provide the student with knowledge and basic skills in firefighting, to respond first at the scene of accident or disaster, and the procedures to follow to provide safety for themselves and the public when confronted with a hazardous situation. This is part one of a two-semester academy that prepares students for work as firefighters.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • EDM 36312 - Fire Academy II

    Credit Hours: 3 to 6
    The Fire Science program meets state requirements for Firefighter I and II, First Responder, and Hazardous Material as recognized by the Missouri Division of Fire Safety. This is the second part of a two-semester academy, designed to prepare students to operate safely on the fireground.

    Prerequisite(s): EDM 36311  or permission of the instructor.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3 to 6
  

  • EDM 36314 - Wilderness First Aid

    Credit Hours: 1 to 6
    This course focuses on the medical assistance needed for individuals in an austere environment where traditional emergency medical services may not be available. Students will learn about patient assessment, symptoms, treatment, movement, and other strategies that can be utilized with minimal tools and technology.

    Typically Offered: SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 6
  

  • EDM 36401 - Professional Development in Emergency and Disaster Management

    Credit Hours: 1
    This course is designed to meet the needs of students preparing the graduate and start their search for full-time employment. This course will walk through the process from searching for employment, to portfolio development, to application procedures, to the interview and beyond.

    Prerequisite(s): EDM 36305  and EDM 36309 .
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • EDM 36402 - Exercise and Simulation Design

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    This course discusses the effective planning, construction, and implementation of emergency management exercises and simulations. Focus will be given to the range of techniques used by relevant organizations, as well as what techniques are most effective according to current research.

    Prerequisite(s): EDM 36305  and EDM 36309 .
    Corequisite(s): EDM 36403 
    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • EDM 36403 - Operational Leadership in EDM

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    This focuses on the theory and practice necessary for effective leadership in emergency and disaster management. The course will provide a background on common theories of leadership, as well as learning in the field through exercises and simulations.

    Prerequisite(s): EDM 36305  and EDM 36309 .
    Corequisite(s): EDM 36402 
    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • EDM 36450 - Homeland Security and Defense

    Credit Hours: 3
    This is a core course in the interdisciplinary major and minor in Emergency and Disaster Management. America has been practicing various types of homeland security since the establishment of the country. However, the current concept of homeland security and the establishment of a formal Department of Homeland Security are direct results of the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001. This course examines homeland security (and to a lesser extent homeland defense) from both a technical view and through a critical lens. The foundation of the course is the application of higher order and critical thinking skills.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3

    Repeatable: Repeatable for new experiences up to 12 credit hours.

Credit Hours: 1 to 6

  • EDUC 61252 - Pluralism in a Multicultural Society

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to impart a knowledge of and sensitivity to issues which foster potential prejudices and discrimination. These issues include race, national origin, sexism, religion, handicaps (physical and mental), economic position and others. Methodology in humanizing the curriculum, sensitivity issues, historical perspectives, and means by which dehumanizing biases may be recognized in instructional materials also shall be included in the content of this course.

Credit Hours: 3

Candidates will explore inter- and intra-disciplinary approaches to curriculum integration including content literacy and sub-disciplines within a single content area. Structures and strategies for integration will be emphasized.

  • EDUC 61324 - Intervention & Assessment: Literacy, Special Populations, and English Language

    Credit Hours: 2
    Candidates in this course will investigate multiple assessment instruments to identify reading and writing challenges and plan effective instruction and intervention for middle school, secondary, and K-12 students. Emphasis is placed upon understanding the major causes of reading, writing, and processing challenges and using a variety of assessment strategies to plan instruction with interventions. Candidates in this course will learn evidence-based strategies of intervention to support children in the general education classroom as well as the special education and ELL classrooms.

Credit Hours: 2

    Corequisite(s): EDUC 61470 , EDUC 61472 , or EDUC 61474 .
Credit Hours: 2
  

  • EDUC 61461 - School and Society

    Credit Hours: 2 to 3
    A survey of the sociological, multicultural, and literacy needs in schools in our society today. Because one of the most significant issues facing our schools and our society today is the inability of students to meet proficiency levels in literacy, an emphasis of this course will be instructional interventions for students with reading deficits.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Corequisite(s): Secondary students: EDUC 61360 , EDUC 61520 , and Content Methods the semester prior to student teaching.
Credit Hours: 2 to 3
  

  • EDUC 61462 - School and Society Seminar

    Credit Hours: 1
    Course is to be taken during the student teaching semester and is a continuation of EDUC 61461  with many of those topics re-addressed as students are actively engaged in student teaching experiences. The preparatory work done in the 2-hour credit course will serve as a foundation for discussion, for application, for reflection and for alteration.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1

Also included are an introduction to innovative instructional methods, a unit on use of instructional technology and a unit on evaluative procedures.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
