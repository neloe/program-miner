English: Literature
  

  • ENGL 10224 - Multiethnic Literature of the United States

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR LITR 105

    This course focuses on the study and understanding of the literary works of ethnically-diverse peoples of the United States. Students will read texts by American writers with diverse racial and ethnic histories, with attention paid to the social and cultural contexts from which these works arise.

    Typically Offered: FA


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10226 - Modern and Contemporary World Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR LITR 200M

    This course examines literature of the twentieth century and later. The major focus of the course will be on world literature other than that produced in the United States or Great Britain. The course includes close critical reading of a variety of genres as well as attention to the cultural contexts from which the literature derives.

    Typically Offered: SP


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10230 - Foundations of Literary Study

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides a foundation in literary history and criticism for English majors and minors. Students will practice the technique of careful, close reading of literary texts and will develop as writers of literary criticism and readers of literary theory. Students should take this course within the first year of their pursuit of an English major or minor.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10305 - The Literary Critic’s Craft

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course examining the practice of literary criticism over time and especially in the past century. This course shows such criticism being used in reflection on major texts during what have been on-going debates about the literature scholar’s discipline.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10306 - U.S. Literature to 1865

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR LITR 101A

    This course charts the development of the literature of the United States from the Indigenous and pre-colonial period to the late-19th century through a range of authors, literary movements, and genres.

    Typically Offered: FA


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10307 - U.S. Literature: 1865 to Present

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR LITR 101B

    This course traces the development of the literature of the United States from the Civil War and Reconstruction era to the present through a range of authors, literary movements, and genres.

    Typically Offered: SP


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10318 - British Literature to the 18th Century

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR LITR 102A

    This course traces British literature from the Anglo-Saxon period into the late 18th century through a range of authors, historical and literary developments, and genres.

    Typically Offered: FA


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10319 - British Literature Since the 18th Century

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR LITR 102B

    This course follows the evolution of British literature from the late 18th century to the present through a range of authors, literary movements, and genres.

    Typically Offered: SP


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10325 - Special Studies

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    An intensive study of a literary theme, a genre or an approach to writing, language, or literature. The subject matter or emphasis will change each semester.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat this course with a change in topic.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • ENGL 10331 - The Bible and Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    Study of Bible stories as key narratives of Middle Eastern and Western culture, both in their original Biblical contexts and in adapted versions prepared by literary writers of many later eras. Tales from both Old and New Testaments are featured, along with adaptations of them by later poets, playwrights, and novelists.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10340 - Cultural Studies in Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will focus on the literature of ideologies, identities, and/or social movements. The subject matter of emphasis will change each semester. Potential subjects include the following: The Harlem Renaissance; Contemporary Queer Literature; and Literature and Regional Identities.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat this course with a change in topic for a maximum of nine hours.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10341 - Literature and Film

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course will examine the interrelationship between film and literature by studying various literary forms – novels, short stories, non-fiction and poetry – alongside related motion pictures and genres.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10111  and ENGL 10112 , or ENGL 10115 ; and ENGL 10220 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10343 - Survey of Women’s Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of women’s literature in all genres from the Middle Ages to the present. Readings may include Kempe, Lanyer, Behn, Wollstonecraft, Chopin, Gilman, Woolf, Lessing, Atwood, Kingston, Silko and Walker.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10351 - Science Fiction Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    An examination of the history, thematic development, and artistic and social impact of science fiction, especially in print but not exclusive of other literary media. A fundamental goal of the course will be to interrogate how science fiction, which exemplifies the broader genre of fantastic literature, is not only fantastic but also literature.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10111  or ENGL 10115 ; and ENGL 10220 .
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10372 - Introduction to Shakespeare

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to Shakespeare’s most popular and/or important plays and poems, including the sonnets, Romeo and Juliet, The Taming of the Shrew and such works as The Tempest, Hamlet, Macbeth, A Midsummer Night’s Dream, Richard III and Venus and Adonis.

    Typically Offered: FA, Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10428 - Directed Reading in Literature

    Credit Hours: 1 to 6
    Tutorial for individual student projects. Instructor’s permission required.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat this course with a change in topic.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 6
  

  • ENGL 10520 - Studies in Early British Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of genres, figures, and/or issues from British literature of the Medieval and Renaissance periods. Specific subject matter and emphasis of course will change each semester. Possible topics include Middle English Literature, which would include Medieval writers such as Chaucer, Margery Kempe, and Sir Thomas Malory; Renaissance Drama, examining the works of authors such as Christopher Marlowe, Elizabeth Cary, and Shakespeare; or intensive focus on the works of a specific writer such as the Pearl Poet, Edmund Spenser, or John Milton.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with a change in topic.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10530 - Studies in British Literature, 18th Century-Present

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of genres, figures, and/or issues from British literature of the past 300 years. Specific subject matter and emphasis will change each semester. Possible topics include Literature of the Romantic Movement, examining works by authors such as Emily Bronte, William Wordsworth, Mary Shelley, and Lord Byron; The Victorian Age, featuring writers such as Charles Dickens, Elizabeth Barrett Browning, and Rudyard Kipling; or Twentieth-Century British Fiction, including writers such as Joseph Conrad, Virginia Woolf, James Joyce, and Anthony Burgess.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with a change in topic.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10540 - Studies in Multiculturalism and World Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    An intensive study of World Literature or Multicultural Literature. Specific subject matter and emphasis of course will change each semester. Possible topics might include Literature of the Harlem Renaissance, with works by authors such as Hughes, Toomer, McKay, Hurston, and Bontemps; The Development of the Modern Novel, examining novels by authors such as Petronius, Boccaccio, Cervantes, Voltaire, Austen, Dostoevsky, Woolf, Beckett, Robbe-Grillet, Morrison, and Achebe; or Anglophone Literature: Fiction of the Post-Colonial World, including works by Rushdie, Naipaul, Gordimer, Lessing, Coatzee, White, Atwood, Munro, and Achebe.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with a change in topic.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10550 - Studies in American Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    An intensive study of topics in American Literature. Specific subject matter and emphasis will change each semester. Sample topics include American Romanticism, with such authors as Emerson, Thoreau, Fuller, Whitman, and Dickinson; The Small Town in American Literature, with works by Hawthorne, Twain, Anderson, Lewis, Gaines, Lee, King, Morrison, and Faulkner; American Modernism and the Long Poem, examining the long works of Whitman, Eliot, Williams, H.D. Stevens, Seamus Heaney, and Rita Dove.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with a change in topic.

Credit Hours: 3

English: Pedagogy
  

  • ENGL 10321 - Teaching Reading in Middle and Secondary English Classrooms

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course addresses theoretical positions, instructional frameworks, and assessment approaches for teaching reading. Students studying to be middle and secondary English teachers will approach reading from multiple frameworks - including reading as information processing, reading as skills/strategies, reading as social-cultural construction, reading as inquiry, and reading as engagement - and consider instruction and assessment grounded in research-based best practices.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10322 - Introduction to the Teaching of Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    Strategies for effective teaching of writing, with some background on writing research and theory.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10380 - Introduction to Writing Center Studies

    Credit Hours: 1
    Introduces students to the field of Writing Center Studies and peer tutoring skills, including theoretical and practical applications.

    Corequisite(s): Writing Center Employment
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • ENGL 10580 - Methods in Secondary School English

    Credit Hours: 3
    Study and practice in strategies for effective teaching of the English language arts in secondary schools. Includes materials, methods, planning, assessment and evaluation, and current issues and trends in teaching the English language arts.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10590 - Young Adult Literature

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of literature for grades 7 through 12 and the ways that literature can be taught in the classroom. The course teaches students to judge young adult literature by accepted and respected critical standards and to understand what makes the literature suitable for students.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3

English: Writing
  

  • ENGL 10101 - English as a Foreign Language

    Credit Hours: 3
    For the individual needs of students whose native language is not English. Meets five times per week. Does not fulfill the ENGL 10110  or ENGL 10111  requirement.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10110 - Introduction to College Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    A college-level composition course designed to develop skills in prewriting, drafting, editing, and rewriting. Recommended for students who scored below 40 on the Directed Self Placement questionnaire.

    Prerequisite(s): Completion of the Directed Self Placement questionnaire.
    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10111 - Composition I: Writing in Academic Contexts

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR ENGL 100

    Study and practice in reading and writing texts towards development of college-level academic literacies. Students will engage through writing and reading in knowledge-based inquiry and sustained critical thinking for the purpose of better understanding writing as a skill and a subject of study. Concepts taught will include: genre awareness,  academic rhetoric, research and documentation, and use of technology as it relates to writing.

    Recommended Prerequisite(s): A grade of C or better in ENGL 10110  or a score of at least 40 in the Directed Self Placement questionnaire.

    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10112 - Composition II: Writing in Public and Personal Contexts

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR ENGL 200

    Study and practice of writing as a form of engagement in public and personal contexts. Builds writing skills through the investigation of communities outside of the university, while working towards transferring skills gained in ENGL 10111  (rhetoric, research, genre awareness).  Students will investigate and write in genres other than academic essays, using appropriate conventions, including visual rhetoric and other media.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10111  

    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10115 - Accelerated Composition

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR ENGL 100

    Combines the course material of ENGL 10111 - Composition I: Writing in Academic Contexts  and ENGL 10112 - Composition II: Writing in Public and Personal Contexts  into one semester. Skills covered will include those required for academic literacies, such as academic rhetoric, and research, as well as those applicable to writing and reading beyond the university. Students will complete an extensive writing project which may incorporate various genres and media as well as primary and secondary research. For further information, see the course descriptions for ENGL 10111  and ENGL 10112 . Satisfactory completion of this course fulfills all six hours of The Northwest Core requirements for Written Communication. Three, GPA bearing, credit hours will be applied for ENGL 10115 . Three, non-GPA bearing, credit hours will be applied as well.

    Prerequisite(s): Completion of the Directed Self Placement questionnaire.
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): A score of 60 or higher on the Directed Self Placement questionnaire.

    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10203 - Foundations of Rhetorical Study

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course introduces students to major theoretical concepts in rhetoric, writing, and communication. The theoretical foundation provided in this course helps students explore the intricate connections between language, knowledge, power, and value, tracing their historical development across diverse cultures and periods. Additionally, they will acquire the skills to conceptualize and practice argumentation and apply their knowledge and skills in complex and rigorous written works.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10112  or ENGL 10115 .
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10210 - Introduction to Creative Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    A multi-genre introduction to creative writing. Emphasis is on producing and responding to creative work in various genres including poetry, fiction and creative nonfiction.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10252 - AI Prompt Engineering

    Credit Hours: 3
    Building upon foundational AI concepts, this course explores the art and science of prompt engineering. Through iterative practice and real-world scenarios, students will learn key prompt engineering techniques and creatively repurpose those techniques to develop their own methods and methodologies to effectively guide generative artificial intelligence (Gen-AI) models to generate outputs tailored to specific audiences, purposes, and contexts.

    Prerequisite(s): CSIS 44122 and ENGL 10112 or ENGL 10115 (or equivalent credit).
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10312 - Creative Writing: Creative Nonfiction

    Credit Hours: 3
    Composition of original creative nonfiction. Students have the opportunity to study published works, write original works, critique the works of others and consult with the professor about their works on a regular basis.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10112  or ENGL 10115 .
    Typically Offered: Alt Years

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10313 - Creative Writing: Fiction

    Credit Hours: 3
    Composition of original short stories or other forms of prose fiction. Students have the opportunity to study published works, write original works, critique the works of others and consult with the professor about their works on a regular basis.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10112  or ENGL 10115 .
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10314 - Creative Writing: Poetry

    Credit Hours: 3
    Composition of original poetry and study of poetic forms and techniques. Students have the opportunity to study published works, write original works, critique the works of others and consult with the professor about their works on a regular basis.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10112  or ENGL 10115 .
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10315 - Professional and Technical Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course provides instruction in the rhetorical concerns of professional and technical writing, such as writing process, ethics, document design, and awareness of audience, purpose, genre, and context. It also provides individual practice with common professional and technical genres, and may include practice with collaboration in activities, assignments, and larger projects.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10112  or ENGL 10115  or permission of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10316 - Creative Writing: Playwriting

    Credit Hours: 3
    Composition of original short plays, dramatic scenes and monologues. The course provides an examination of the elements, discipline and art of playwriting through analysis of established plays and creation of original short plays, dramatic scenes and monologues.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10210  or THEA 43225  or permission of instructor.
    Cross-Listed: THEA 43316  

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10405 - Senior Seminar

    Credit Hours: 3
    A capstone course focused on further practice in researching and writing about literature and the teaching of literature. In addition to compiling a portfolio of materials to aid in application to graduate schools or teaching positions, students will have the opportunity to practice public presentation of their writing in order to synthesize various elements of critical thinking, reflect on major texts and demonstrate research abilities as well as analytic skills in the discipline.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10580  (for B.S.Ed.-Minor Required students only) OR ENGL 10230  (for all other majors).
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10220  , ENGL 10224  , ENGL 10306  , ENGL 10307  , ENGL 10318  , ENGL 10319  , ENGL 10372  .
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10410 - Professional Portfolio Preparation

    Credit Hours: 3
    A capstone class for B.S. in English students, focused on preparing a professional portfolio to facilitate application for jobs in writing, editing and publishing.

    Prerequisite(s): Senior standing or instructor permission.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10414 - Approaches to Creative Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    An intermediate creative writing workshop that investigates issues of craft, form, and theory through reading, writing, and discussion. Students have the opportunity to study published works, write original works, critique the works of others, and consult with the professor about their works on a regular basis. The specific content of the course changes each time the course is offered. Course topics may include Poetic Forms, Hybridity, Prose Poetry, Critical Reading for Creative Writers, Flash Fiction, Villainy and Bad Behavior in Fiction, The Novella, Approaches to Revision, Exploring Subtext in Fiction, Lyric Essay Forms, and Intentional Experience Essays.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10210  or ENGL 10312  or ENGL 10313  or ENGL 10314  or ENGL 10316  or instructor permission.
    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with a change in topic.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10419 - Directed Writing

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    Independent work in creative or expository writing. Instructor’s permission required.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat this course with a change in topic.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • ENGL 10430 - Digital Literacy

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course on the reading and composing of digital texts including study of genre and rhetoric in a number of digital environments. The specific content of the course will vary each semester, with topics such as Internet Rhetoric, Emerging Online Genres, Writing and Online Identity, or The Digital Humanities. This course does not require coding knowledge.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10112  or ENGL 10115 .
    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with change of topic.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10495 - Internship in Writing and Research

    Credit Hours: 1 to 8
    Internships (paid and unpaid) will be offered as available or as located by students. Internships require a written proposal at the time of registration and permission of the instructor and school chairperson. Fifty work hours are required for each hour of academic credit. A maximum of three credit hours may be applied to the major or minor.

    Prerequisite(s): Junior standing and three hours of writing courses at the 300 level or above.
    Repeatable: Course may be repeated for a maximum of 11 hours.

Credit Hours: 1 to 8
  

  • ENGL 10512 - Publication Skills

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course covers major skills required in publishing, provides an overview of the publishing industry, and prepares students for work in areas of technical writing, magazine editing and freelance copyediting.

    Prerequisite(s): Junior or graduate student standing, or instructor permission.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10515 - Advanced Professional and Technical Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course develops students’ understanding of and practice in the rhetorical, practical, and ethical concerns in professional and technical writing contexts. Furthermore, this course challenges, complicates, and expands students’ previous understanding with more theoretical and global writing concerns. In the course, students practice writing both in individual assignments and research projects, which may include focusing more specifically on a particular professional discipline, industry, or area and/or produce document-based solutions for professional problems.

    Prerequisite(s): ENGL 10315  or instructor approval.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10560 - Advanced Creative Writing

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced course in the study and writing of original poetry, fiction, or creative non-fiction. Specific subject matter and emphasis will change each semester.

    Prerequisite(s): A 300- or 400-level creative writing class or consent of instructor.
    Repeatable: Students may repeat the course with a change in topic.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • ENGL 10570 - Advanced Studies in Writing and Rhetoric

    Credit Hours: 3
    Covers various issues in writing and rhetoric. Specific subject matter will change each semester. Possible topics might include: the history of rhetoric, contemporary rhetorical theory, composition theory, genre theory, research methods in composition, and qualitative research methodology.

    Repeatable: Students may repeat this course with a change of topic.

Credit Hours: 3

Finance
  

  • FIN 53120 - Personal Money Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to the basic principles and practice of successful personal money management. The life cycle of personal financial planning and management will be discussed.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53311 - Business Law I

    Credit Hours: 3
    Fundamental principles of law in relation to the legal environment of business, including procedure and evidence, business ethics, court systems, contracts, torts, administrative law and constitutional law.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53312 - Business Law II

    Credit Hours: 3
    Fundamental principles of law in relation to business, including agency, negotiable instruments, secured transactions, bankruptcy, partnerships, corporations and other business entities.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53320 - Insurance and Risk

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the principles of insurance, including the various types of insurance coverages such as home, auto, life and health, as well as principles of risk management.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53324 - Fundamentals of Business Finance

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the financial structure of corporations, principles and techniques used in financial analysis and decision-making.

    Prerequisite(s): A grade of C or better in ACCT 51202  and MATH 17116  or MATH 17117  or MATH 17118  or MATH 17119  or MATH 17120  or consent of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53325 - Investment Principles

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the principles of investment and investment vehicles.

    Prerequisite(s): FIN 53324  with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53326 - Investment Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course dealing with fixed income, derivatives, and foreign exchange.

    Prerequisite(s): FIN 53324  and FIN 53325  both with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53327 - Intermediate Financial Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    An in-depth study of financial theory and practices including such topics as capital structure, dividend policy, capital budgeting, asset pricing and working capital management.

    Prerequisite(s): FIN 53324  with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53419 - Alternative Investments

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of alternative investments including such assets as private equity, hedge funds, commodities, and real estate.

    Prerequisite(s): FIN 53324  with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53420 - Financial Institutions

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course which deals with structure, functions, policies and decision-making within financial institutions.

    Prerequisite(s): FIN 53324  with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53421 - Selected Cases in Finance

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced case study course dealing with the management of corporate finance.

    Prerequisite(s): FIN 53327  with a minimum grade of C.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • FIN 53425 - Intern in Finance

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Finance majors may, with approval from the School Chairperson, enroll in an internship section. This is recommended for students with junior or senior standing.

    Repeatable: This course is repeatable for new experience.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • FIN 53429 - Independent Study in Finance and Insurance

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Finance majors may, with approval from the School Chairperson, enroll in an independent study section. The approval must be received from the School Chairperson and should be received during the term preceding the enrollment.

    Repeatable: This course is repeatable with topics changing.

Credit Hours: 1 to 3

Foods and Nutrition
  

  • NUTR 47230 - Food Science

    Credit Hours: 4
    A laboratory course with emphasis on the study of scientific principles of food preparation and on criteria for recognition and evaluation of standard products.

    Hours per week: Three hours of lecture and 3 hours of lab per week.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • NUTR 47232 - Food Service Management

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introductory food service course emphasizing food service sanitation and requiring application of principles of food buying and food preparation to menu planning, meal preparation and service. Completion of ServSafe certification.

    Prerequisite(s): NUTR 47230  with minimum grade of C or by permission.
    Hours per week: Three hours lecture.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • NUTR 47234 - Nutrition and Diet Behavior

    Credit Hours: 3
    An entry-level nutrition course which provides basic nutrition information and the science behind nutrition and diet behavior. Coursework will include a self-assessment of nutrient intake utilizing computer analysis.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • NUTR 47240 - Introduction to Dietetics

    Credit Hours: 2
    Exploration of the dietetics profession. Introduces the professional organization and structure. Covers credentialing and the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics Code of Ethics, scope of dietetics practice, Northwest dietetics handbook and requirements to obtain verification statement. Clarifying the roles and requirements of different nutrition professionals and the interdisciplinary relationships in various practice settings.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • NUTR 47332 - Quantity Food Preparation

    Credit Hours: 2
    A lecture course in basic quantity food preparation with emphasis on principles of preparation, identification of quality products, standardized recipes, safety and sanitation, care and supervision of food service equipment, organization of work space, work simplification and preparation of food.

    Prerequisite(s): NUTR 47232 
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • NUTR 47333 - Quantity Food Preparation Laboratory

    Credit Hours: 2
    Provides laboratory experience in quantity food preparation.

    Hours per week: Lab hours variable per week.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • NUTR 47334 - Lifespan Nutrition

    Credit Hours: 3
    Fundamentals of nutrition and disease states throughout the lifespan will be the focus of the course with information also provided on community programs that impact each stage of the lifespan.   • NUTR 47433 - Medical Nutrition Therapy I

    Credit Hours: 4
    This course is designed to develop skills in the use of clinical nutrition in the prevention and treatment of diet-related health problems. This course includes the assessment of nutritional status of individuals, and medical nutrition therapy for various disease states such as disorders of the upper and lower GI, CVD, Hypertension, Type 2 Diabetes, nutritional concerns during and after pregnancy, complete overview of nutritional support (enteral and parenteral nutrition and tube feeding), weight management, disordered eating. This course will also allow the student to grow in their ability to use the Nutrition Care Process, ADIME and SOAP charting.

    Prerequisite(s): NUTR 47234
