Geography
  

  • GEOG 32441 - European Landscapes

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced regional and thematic course examining the nations and regions of the European cultural area excluding Russia.

    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32455 - Applied Geospatial Data Analytics

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course designed to apply various geospatial data analytic techniques to help students to make data-driven decisions in fields such as business, public health, agriculture, urban planning, recreation, and environmental management. Students will learn how to acquire and manage spatial data from various sources, preprocess and analyze the data to identify patterns and visualize the results using modern GIS software tools to solve real-world problems in diverse fields.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOG 32365  or permission of the instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32462 - Advanced Cartography and Geovisualization

    Credit Hours: 3
    This advanced course delves into the dynamic world of cartography and geovisualization, equipping students with the skills and knowledge to create compelling and informative maps and visualizations. Building upon foundational cartographic principles, students will explore advanced techniques, tools, and technologies to create maps and interactive geospatial visualizations. Topics include spatial data visualization, 3D cartography, spatio-temporal mapping (dynamic mapping), and open-source mapping techniques.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOG 32365  or permission of instructor.
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32463 - Remote Sensing

    Credit Hours: 3
    Remote sensing is an introductory course designed for the geography and/or geology major interested in analyzing the earth from the atmosphere and outer space.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOG 32201  or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32465 - Introduction to Customized GIS

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course designed to apply state-of-art programming language to customize and automate GIS tasks. Customization of GIS provides users with personalized and specialized functions and interfaces while automation makes daily geoprocessing analysis easier and faster.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOG 32365 
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32499 - Senior Seminar

    Credit Hours: 1
    A capstone course in geography. Course is designed to assess the student’s ability to synthesize and evaluate geographic knowledge as it applies toward professional enhancement and/or further professional development in higher education.

    Prerequisite(s): Senior standing and completion of the Geography Core.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • GEOG 32510 - Geographic Education: Themes and Materials

    Credit Hours: 2
    Designed for elementary or secondary teachers wishing to incorporate an instructional unit in geography in either the social science or science curriculum.

    Typically Offered: SU

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • GEOG 32511 - Special Topics

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Will be offered according to student needs and interest. Each offering will be designed to apply the latest information or techniques pertaining to a timely topic in geography/GIS.

    Prerequisite(s): 12 hours of geography and permission of instructor.
    Repeatable: Repeatable for new area of study to earn no more than six credits total.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • GEOG 32521 - Geopolitics

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced course in geopolitics with emphasis on fundamental principles and their application to the major regions and nations of the world today.

    Prerequisite(s): Junior standing or permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32522 - Cities and Urban Design

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced course offering an in-depth study of characteristics of cities and problems faced in urban environments. The course emphasizes how historical, social, and cultural factors shape cities.

    Prerequisite(s): Junior standing or permission of the instructor.
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOG 32565 - Advanced Geographic Information Systems

    Credit Hours: 3
    Builds on the techniques and concepts learned in GEOG 32365 - Geographic Information Systems . Stresses research and project design strategies and advanced analytical techniques using geographic information systems to solve spatial problems.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOG 32365  
    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3

Geology
  

  • GEOL 27110 - General Geology

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR GEOL 100L

    A study of Earth as a dynamic planet, with a focus on internal and external processes. Common themes include: earth materials, earthquakes, volcanism, mountain building, streams, groundwater, and surficial processes. When possible, these processes are placed in the context of human uses and other impacts.

    Corequisite(s): GEOL 27111 
    Hours per week: Three one hour lectures per week.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27111 - General Geology Laboratory

    Credit Hours: 1
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR GEOL 100L

    Hands-on’ laboratory instruction in identification of common minerals and rocks, the uses of topographic maps, surface and subsurface processes, and water and related resources. Laboratory methods may include physical identification, pen and paper analyses, and computer-based data analyses.

    Corequisite(s): GEOL 27110 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • GEOL 27114 - General Earth Science

    Credit Hours: 3
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR PHYS 110LES

    This course focuses on a general introductory survey of the earth sciences of physical geography, geology, oceanography, climatology and meteorology. The connection between the various components of the earth system will be analyzed. 

    Corequisite(s): GEOL 27115  
    Hours per week: Three hours of lecture.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27115 - General Earth Science Laboratory

    Credit Hours: 1
    MOTR Equivalent: MOTR PHYS 110LES

    This course gives students a chance to apply, through written exercises, the concepts discussed in GEOL 27114 .

    Corequisite(s): GEOL 27114 
    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU


    https://journeytocollege.mo.gov/finish/transfer-credit/core-42/
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • GEOL 27130 - Information Technology and Science

    Credit Hours: 3
    Course studying the basics of computer systems and software used at Northwest as well as the professional world. Topics reviewed will include studies of PC-based computer systems, use of the Northwest network system, effective use of Internet resources and common productivity software (such as office programs, suites, e-mail, Web-course) with particular emphasis on applications to science and technology.

    Hours per week: Three lecture hours each week.

    Typically Offered: SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27212 - Historical Geology

    Credit Hours: 4
    This course employs a study of deep time, important rock units, plate tectonics and the fossil and stratigraphic records to illuminate the physical and biological evolution of Earth. Special emphasis is placed on the formation and subsequent physical evolution of North America, and the origin and evolution of life as understood from evidence in the fossil record (fauna and flora).

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115 .
    Hours per week: Three hours of lecture and a two-hour laboratory.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27214 - Gemology

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to gemology including description, identification, and assessment of key physical qualities of gemstones and their subsitutes.

    Typically Offered: Alt Semesters, Alt Years

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27220 - Mineralogy

    Credit Hours: 4
    A survey of chemical and physical mineralogy including identification of minerals, structural grouping of minerals, occurrence of silicate minerals, as well as deposits and uses of non-silicate minerals.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115 , and CHEM 24114   and CHEM 24115   (must obtain a grade of C or higher) or permission of the instructor.
    Hours per week: Three lectures and one two-hour lab per week.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27305 - Practicum in Teaching Laboratory

    Credit Hours: 1
    To assist faculty in laboratory situations in classes like GEOL 27111 , GEOL 27115 , GEOL 27220 , GEOL 27212   and for preparation for teaching positions in graduate school.

    Prerequisite(s): Successful completion of the course, permission of the laboratory instructor and school chairperson and a major or minor in geology or geography or earth science education.
    Hours per week: Two hours of lab.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • GEOL 27320 - Geology Field Trip

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    This course is designed to introduce students to the use of basic field investigation to understand geologic processes from evidence in the rocks. Field trip localities are selected to provide an introduction to minerals, rock types, fossils, structures, landforms, or other geologic phenomena that are not found locally.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115  or permission of the instructor.
    Repeatable: Repeatable for new area of study.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 1 to 3
  

  • GEOL 27326 - Geology of the Natural Parks

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the geologic features of the 38 U.S. National Parks including consideration of their causes and changes.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115 .
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27335 - Physical Oceanography

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the oceans including the physical properties of the oceans and marine geology.

    Prerequisite(s): Completion of general education science requirement.
    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27340 - Introduction to Hydrogeology

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of practical hydrogeologic principles typically used in the environmental consulting industry. Topics studied cover the occurrence, movement, quality, contamination and management of groundwater.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115  and MATH 17118 .
    Hours per week: Two lecture hours and one two-hour laboratory session per week.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27345 - Optical Mineralogy

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the optical properties of non-opaque minerals through use of the petrographic (polarizing) microscopes utilizing oil immersion and thin section methods, and application of refractometry toward mineral identification.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27220  
    Hours per week: Two one-hour lectures and one two-hour lab per week.

    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27360 - Environmental Geology

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of geology focusing on the inter-relationship between man and his environment and resulting impacts to the Earth’s systems. Topics investigated include natural processes as hazards, man’s impacts on earth resources as well as contemporary environmental issues (e.g., climate change, sea rise, pollution, earthquakes, etc.)

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115 .
    Hours per week: Three lecture hours each week.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27369 - Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology

    Credit Hours: 4
    A study of igneous and metamorphic rocks including: identification (hand sample and petrography), mineral compositions, fabrics/textures, occurrences, genesis, and classifications.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27220  or permission of instructor.
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27450  has traditionally been reccomended to be taken before or concurrently with Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology
    Hours per week: Three one-hour lectures and one two-hour laboratory per week.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27415 - Internship in Geology

    Credit Hours: 1 to 6
    Outreach efforts by students and faculty often generate internship opportunities with environmental consulting firms, natural resource companies and government agencies. Internship courses require approval of the instructor, school chairperson, and employer offering the opportunity along with a written proposal from the student at the time of registration. The student enrolls in the appropriate number of credit hours for the work load of the internship. Typically, 160 work hours are worth 3 credit hours.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for additional experience

    Typically Offered: FA, SP, SU

Credit Hours: 1 to 6
  

  • GEOL 27423 - Economic Geology

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of the origin and distribution of economic ore, critical minerals, aggregates, and energy resources (oil and gas, geothermal energy, etc.). Major ore deposits are discussed, with a focus on exploration geology, understanding inter-connected geologic processes involved in developing natural resources, mining, and various economic, environmental, social, and governance impacts.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27220  
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27369  GEOL 27424  
    Hours per week: Three one-hour lectures.

    Typically Offered: Odd SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27424 - Geochemistry

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course focuses on theoretical and applied geochemical approaches to understanding geologic systems. Emphasis is placed on acid-base and solubility equilibria, and stable and radiogenic isotope chemistry, primarily as related to geochronology and the investigation of shallow crustal rocks.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27220 
    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27440 - Sedimentology and Stratigraphy

    Credit Hours: 4
    A comprehensive study of the principles and processes involved in sedimentary petrology and stratigraphy covering sediment production, transportation, deposition, and lithification, depositional environments, litho-/chrono-/bio-/sequence- stratigraphic concepts, and correlation. Students learn to apply fundamental techniques for environmental interpretations through lecture-laboratory exercises.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27212  
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27369  
    Hours per week: Three one-hour lectures and one two-hour lab.

    Typically Offered: Even FA

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27450 - Structural Geology

    Credit Hours: 4
    The study of the architecture of rock units in Earth’s crust as a result of deformation associated with Earth’s tectonic forces. Subject matter includes stress-strain relationships, folds, faults, rock fabrics, and plate tectonics. Laboratory techniques include strike and dip, scaled drawings and construction techniques, geologic maps and cross-sections, isopach maps, structural maps, and stereonets.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27212  and GEOL 27369  and one mathematics course that incorporates trigonometry.
    Hours per week: Five hours of lecture and laboratory per week.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27455 - Geologic Field Methods

    Credit Hours: 3
    Basic geological surveying techniques will be studied in the field. Notebook procedures and reporting will be stressed.

    Prerequisite(s): Geology Major or minor with 20 hours in geology.
    Typically Offered: SU

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27498 - Senior Seminar

    Credit Hours: 1
    A capstone course in Geology. A student will examine current geologic research results and techniques, investigate employment opportunities, and assess his/her fundamental understanding of geology.

    Prerequisite(s): Senior standing as a Geology, Environmental Geology or Unified Science in Earth Science major, and a GPA of at least 2.0 in the major.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 1
  

  • GEOL 27499 - Senior Paper

    Credit Hours: 1
Credit Hours: 1
  

  • GEOL 27501 - Special Topics in Geology

    Credit Hours: 1 to 4
    Will be offered according to student needs and interest. Each offering will be designed to incorporate the latest information pertaining to a timely topic in geology. Topics may include: rock and mineral origins and classifications, groundwater, energy, age of dinosaurs, fossils and the history of life, volcanoes and earthquakes, and glacial geology.

    Prerequisite(s): One year of college-level science or permission of the instructor.
    Repeatable: Repeatable for new area of study.

Credit Hours: 1 to 4
  

  • GEOL 27510 - Geomorphology

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of landforms, focusing on the description, recognition, classification and formation processes. Topics investigated include the origin and nature of geomorphologic processes shaping different landforms, along with the influence of geologic controls, climate and other factors impacting these processes.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111  or GEOL 27114  and GEOL 27115 .
    Hours per week: Two lecture hours and one two-hour laboratory session per week.

    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27515 - Environmental Regulations

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to federal and state regulations and major issues associated with the environment acts, including air quality, water quality, endangered species, critical zones, the disposal of hazardous waste, environmental justice, etc. Additionally, project development at local, state, federal, and international levels (e.g., Environmental Impact Statement and Environmental Impact Assessment / Design) will be discussed. 

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27360  or BIOL 04420  or GEOG 32401  or BIOL 04368   or permission of instructor.
    Hours per week: Three lecture hours each week.

    Typically Offered: Odd SP, Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • GEOL 27520 - Geology Field Trip (Advanced Level)

    Credit Hours: 2
    An advanced version of the introductory field course, GEOL 27520  combines basic field investigation with a short project that focuses on a feature of interest from the field trip area. Potential projects include thin section petrography and optical mineralogy, written descriptions of features of interest, reflective essays, or other projects as approved by the course instructor.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27212 
    Repeatable: Repeatable for new area of study.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • GEOL 27540 - Paleontology

    Credit Hours: 4
    A general study of fossils, including classification of plants and animals (paleobiology), development and evolution of prehistoric life (paleobiogeography), paleontological techniques, and use of fossils as: time series, biostratigraphy, depositional environment, paleoenvironmental change, resource evaluation (oil, gas, coal, etc.) and ecological guides. Lab includes study and identification of the major fossil groups. Emphasis is on both vertebrate and invertebrates, with a general review of microfossils, macrofossils, plants (paleobotany), and trace fossils (Ichnofacies). 

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27212  or permission of instructor.
    Hours per week: Three one-hour lectures and one two-hour laboratory.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27590 - Seminar in the Earth Sciences

    Credit Hours: 2
    Seminar and studies of advanced topics in selected fields in geology and other earth sciences.

    Prerequisite(s): GEOL 27110  and GEOL 27111 .
    Hours per week: Two hours per week in lecture, seminar or lab appropriate to the topics.

Credit Hours: 2
  

  • GEOL 27595 - Geologic Data Analysis and Scientific Writing

    Credit Hours: 4
    A studio course introducing different data types used in the geosciences and other disciplines to understand environmental problems, providing skills needed to analyze environmental and geologic data quantitatively. Some of the topics covered will include statistical data analysis including A studio course introducing different data types used in the geosciences and other disciplines to understand environmental problems, providing skills needed to analyze environmental and geologic data quantitatively. Some of the topics covered will include statistical data analysis, including computer-supported geologic applications of elementary statistics, time-series analysis, trend-surface analysis, factor analysis, cluster analysis, analysis of directional data, and regression analysis. Familiarity with basic computer skills is essential.

    Prerequisite(s): MATH 17114 , familiarity with basic computer skills including EXCEL.
    Recommended Prerequisite(s): MATH 17118  
    Typically Offered: Even SP

Credit Hours: 4
  

  • GEOL 27599 - Special Investigations in the Earth Sciences

    Credit Hours: 1 to 3
    Independent studies in the earth sciences including but not limited to research and library studies. Enroll only with consent of school chairperson. Requires written proposal at time of registration.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for additional experience, new material and progression of study.

    Typically Offered: FA, SP

Credit Hours: 1 to 3

  • HIST 33225 - Race and Ethnicity in America

    Focuses on the historical development of ethnicity and race in America. Emphasis will be placed on changing ethnic and racial consciousness and inclusion and exclusion in American society.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33301 - The Historian’s Craft and Its Uses

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is designed to introduce the student to the tools of the craft; to research, to writing and to critical thinking, as well as to the product of the craft–the uses of history–both academic and public, with grant writing experience.

    Typically Offered: SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33302 - Digital Humanities

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course develops students’ application of digital research, presentation, and theoretical tools in the humanities. Focusing on digital history, students will explore current research and apply a methodology to their own projects while broadening their understanding of digital humanities. The course will focus especially on application of digital tools in the humanities by addressing the theories, methods, and application of the diverse digital humanities resulting in student generated projects.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33303 - Global Health in History

    Credit Hours: 3
    Health and illness have shaped human history for millennia. In this course students will explore different global health concerns and crises and how they have shaped social, cultural, and political change.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33305 - The Ancient World

    Credit Hours: 3
    The study of the major events, trends, and personalities of the ancient Mediterranean world focusing on ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, or Rome. Special emphasis is placed on those concepts which are foundational for modern culture and society: identity, gender, religion, conflict.

    Repeatable: The course is repeatable for new experiences up to 6 credit hours.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33306 - The Modern World

    Credit Hours: 3
    A comparative study of the major events, trends, and personalities of the modern world. Special emphasis is placed on the causes and consequences of industrialization, urbanization, globalization, conflict, migration, and nationalism.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33314 - Environmental History of the U.S.

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course introduces students to the study of environmental history in the United States. It explores the role of non-human entities in major social, political, and cultural events and ideas of nature in American cultural history.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33320 - Public History in the U.S.

    Credit Hours: 3
    An introduction to the history, theories, and practices of public history in the U.S. Study will include an overview of fields such as museum education, curatorship, and collections, historic preservation, libraries, and archives. Students will examine the history of these fields, understand how they have evolved in the U.S., and gain practical skills.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33323 - The World at War

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced survey that focuses on the origins, conduct, and outcomes of war in the world history. The course examines the significant wartime and postwar cultural, economic, political, and social consequences of conflict. The content of this course will vary when offered.

    Repeatable: It may be repeated up to six credit hours with the permission of the instructor for new topics.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33325 - Protest and Activism in American History

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examines political, economic, social, and cultural forms of protest and activism in American history from the colonial period to the present. The historical content of this course will vary when offered.

    Repeatable: Repeatable for new experiences up to six credit hours.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33350 - American Military History

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course in the military history of the United States and the relationships of society, politics, diplomacy and economics to the military.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33360 - Women and Gender in U.S. History

    Credit Hours: 3
    Examines the significant cultural, economic, and political developments that shaped the lives of women in the United States from early history to the present and highlights the shifting conceptions and experiences of gender and sexuality.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33362 - The History of Missouri

    Credit Hours: 3
    A brief survey of Spanish and French rule, followed by a study of the history and development of Missouri from the acquisition of the territory by the United States to the present day. Special emphasis is on its historical significance, important figures, government, constitution, current problems, and local history.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33365 - American Religious History

    Credit Hours: 3
    A survey of the diversity of religion in the U.S. focusing on the ways in which American life (politics, economics, foreign policy, social reform) has been influenced by religious movements and ideas in the past, and the challenges offered today by religious pluralism.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33375 - History of Latin America

    Credit Hours: 3
    A brief survey of the history of Latin America designed for the general student, foreign language majors and business administration majors.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33388 - The History of the American Economy

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course is an historical study of the foundations and development of the American economy. It examines the periods of American History from an economic perspective and considers the impact of economic development on politics, society, and culture.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33389 - Early Modern Europe: The West Meets the World

    Credit Hours: 3
    An in-depth examination of the society, culture, and international expansion of Europe from the mid-16th to the mid-18th century, a bloody and often unstable era, but one in which art and science flourished and the world economy and modern political systems were created.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33444 - Practicum in American Local History

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course centers on the study, collection, and presentation of local and regional histories of the American Midwest. Students will be immersed in the study of local U.S. history and the practice of public history work.

    Repeatable: Repeatable, up to 6 hours, for new material.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33480 - Methods in Secondary School Social Studies

    Credit Hours: 3
    A course for prospective teachers in social studies stressing materials, methods, assessments, and techniques in teaching social studies in terms of the needs and problems of secondary education. This course must be taken before student teaching.

    Prerequisite(s): Admittance to Teacher Education.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33490 - Advanced Studies in History

    Credit Hours: 3
    The content of this course will vary when offered. Selected areas of history and historical problems will be given.

    Repeatable: May be repeated once with permission of advisor for new topics.

Credit Hours: 3

  • HIST 33522 - The American Colonies

    Credit Hours: 3
    Provides an in-depth look at colonial development in North America, with an emphasis on the English colonies, how and why they were founded, and how they grew and developed through the revolutionary period. Additional themes may include racial, gender, and religious relationships and conflict, imperial rivalries, and the slow emergence of a more unified colonial identity.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33524 - The Early American Republic

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course examines how the American Revolution transformed thirteen colonies into an independent nation and how the Articles of Confederation and the Constitution laid the foundation for the new country. Emphasis is placed on the culture and society of the period and the era’s tumultuous political and economic events.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33525 - United States Since 1945

    An advanced course which will look at the development of the contemporary United States through an examination of foreign affairs as well as selected social movements within the country.

  • HIST 33532 - History of the Atlantic World

    Credit Hours: 3
    This course asks how history of the early modern period of contact, conquest, and colonization is different if we use an Atlantic lens to frame our questions and analysis. Students will engage with transnational histories that put Africa, Europe, and the Americas in conversation with one another as parts of the same larger historical systems. This course will emphasize the social, cultural, and economic transformation of the region.

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33534 - The Civil War and Reconstruction

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced course in American history which analyzes the conflicting theories and issues of the antebellum period, interprets the coming of the Civil War, the conduct of government, military operations and foreign relations during the war, and problems of the Reconstruction Period.

    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33556 - The Gilded Age and Progressive Era

    Credit Hours: 3
    An advanced course in the era of the United States between the Civil War and World War I, leading to new understandings of modern America. This course will investigate key issues of this era including industrialization, populism, progressivism, migration, race, gender, and identity.

    Typically Offered: Alt SP

Credit Hours: 3
  

  • HIST 33582 - The American West

    Credit Hours: 3
    A study of America’s territorial expansion from the colonial period to the close of the 19th century, including the impact on the nation’s people, institutions, policies and ideas. Special emphasis will be devoted to the conflict between settlers and native people.

    Typically Offered: Alt FA

Credit Hours: 3

    Prerequisite(s): HIST 33301  or by permission of instructor.
    Typically Offered: FA

Credit Hours: 3
